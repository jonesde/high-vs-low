# Gene and diagnostic method patents blocking equipped labs

A clinical or university lab has sequencers, reagents, and trained staff. Before 2013, patents on isolated BRCA genes and on diagnostic correlations still made running the test infringement. Myriad sent cease-and-desist letters to other labs. This is not patient inability to pay. It is a use ban on a technique the lab is equipped to perform.

*Mayo* (2012) and *Myriad* (2013) narrowed what can be claimed. Residual exclusive rights (cDNA, applied methods, proprietary variant databases) remain. The preferential episode is the pre-2013 routing and what survived.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result was, and in leftover form still is, exclusive control of a diagnostic technique by the patentee. Other equipped labs could not lawfully run the test. Patients with money and physicians with labs still had to use the patentee's channel. After *Myriad*, multiple labs entered BRCA testing — evidence that the bottleneck had been the exclusive right, not capacity.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A lab performing a known chemical assay on a purchased sample is ordinary practice of a technique. Facts of nature (a gene sequence as it exists in the body; a correlation between a metabolite and a dose) are not supposed to be owned. Exclusive claims on isolated DNA and on "analyze and compare" methods closed that analog until the Court intervened.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Utility patents on isolated genes | Exclusive right to isolate and test claimed sequences | 35 U.S.C. §§ 101, 271; Myriad's BRCA patents (pre-2013) |
| Method claims on correlations | Exclusive right to use a natural relationship in diagnosis | *Mayo Collaborative Servs. v. Prometheus Labs.*, 566 U.S. 66 (2012) |
| *Myriad* product holding | Isolated genomic DNA is a product of nature, not patent-eligible; cDNA can be | *AMP v. Myriad Genetics, Inc.*, 569 U.S. 576 (2013) |
| Federal Circuit on Myriad methods | "Compare" / "analyze" sequence methods ineligible; some screening methods held eligible | *AMP v. USPTO*, 689 F.3d 1303 (Fed. Cir. 2012) |
| Cease-and-desist / exclusive licensing | Practical enforcement against academic and commercial labs | Myriad licensing practice |

*Mayo*: claims to a method of adjusting drug dose by measuring a metabolite and comparing to a range were ineligible as laws of nature plus conventional steps. Labs that could already run the blood test still infringed the correlation claim until that holding.

*Myriad*: Myriad did not create the BRCA sequences. Isolation is not invention. cDNA (exons only) is not a product of nature and remains eligible. The Court did not decide all method claims; the Federal Circuit had already dropped the diagnostic "analyze/compare" claims.

## Routing

Enabled (pre-2013, and residual): civil infringement against labs that run the test. Closed: ordinary practice of sequencing and interpretation by any equipped lab, until § 101 cut the claims.

Procedural features:

- Exclusive licenses plus letters to Yale, Penn, and others.
- Patent-eligibility is a threshold; it is expensive to litigate. Most labs stopped rather than fund *Myriad*.
- After 2013, trade-secret variant databases and remaining cDNA/method claims are the substitute enclosure.

## Application Evidence

**Capacity was there.** Plaintiffs in *AMP v. Myriad* included pathologists and geneticists whose labs were equipped to sequence BRCA. Myriad's exclusive claims, not missing machines, were the barrier. After the 2013 decision, other labs offered BRCA testing. That before/after is the differential-application evidence.

**Price is not the mechanism on this page.** Myriad's test price was high. The library's technology-access filter is the **lab that could run the assay and still may not**. Patient unaffordability is the resources topic, parked.

Residual: cDNA claims, applied methods that survive *Mayo*, and proprietary interpretation databases. Equipped labs can sequence; they may still lack lawful access to the interpretation layer.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 6 (Individual autonomy).** A physician-lab's ordinary diagnostic work is subordinated to the exclusive claim.
- **Rule 9 (Who defines harm).** The patentee defines unauthorized testing as the harm, not the patient's missed diagnosis at a second lab.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** Isolation of a sequence becomes "invention."
- **Rule 36 (Treatment of the Least).** Patients and smaller labs are the least able to fund eligibility litigation.
- **Rule 42 (Production: distributed or centralized).** Sequencing capacity is distributed. Exclusive claims centralize who may interpret.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
