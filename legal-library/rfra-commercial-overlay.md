# RFRA commercial overlay

*Employment Division v. Smith* (1990) held that a neutral, generally applicable criminal law needs no compelling-interest test ([pla-050](disfavored-practice-criminalization.md)). Congress answered with the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (1993): government may not substantially burden a person's religious exercise unless it shows a compelling interest and the least restrictive means. *City of Boerne v. Flores* (1997) held RFRA cannot commandeer the states. *Burwell v. Hobby Lobby Stores* (2014) held that closely held **for-profit corporations** are RFRA "persons" and need not provide coverage for contraceptives they object to. The analog individual in *Smith* — a Native practitioner, peyote, unemployment — lost. The analog secular closely held firm has no RFRA claim.

This is the hypocrisy page of the wave: **the same vocabulary of religious liberty, two claimant classes**.

This page does not decide contraceptive policy. It names who is a "person" under RFRA.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a statutory shield for organized and commercial claimants against federal burdens. The parties who obtain it are churches (easy RFRA fit), nonprofit ministries, and closely held corporations with a religious objection. Unfavored individuals under generally applicable state criminal law (*Smith*, unless a state RFRA applies) take the residual.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

*Smith*: two members of the Native American Church, fired, denied unemployment. RFRA was written against that result and then used, in the headline case, by a craft-store chain. A secular LLC that objects to a mandate on non-religious grounds does not get the compelling-interest test. State RFRAs (post-*Boerne*) are a patchwork franchise.

*Fulton v. City of Philadelphia*, *Masterpiece Cakeshop*, and *303 Creative* are adjacent public-accommodation / speech routing. They are not this page's holding. They show the same movement: institutional or commercial claimants using Free Exercise and Free Speech to close a generally applicable rule. Short cross-reference, not a second treatise.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| RFRA | Compelling-interest / least-restrictive-means test for federal burdens on religious exercise | [42 U.S.C. §§ 2000bb et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000bb) |
| *Smith* (the problem RFRA names) | Neutral generally applicable laws need no *Sherbert* test | *Employment Division v. Smith*, 494 U.S. 872 (1990) |
| *Boerne* | RFRA exceeds Section 5 as applied to states | *City of Boerne v. Flores*, 521 U.S. 507 (1997) |
| *Hobby Lobby* | Closely held for-profits are RFRA persons | *Burwell v. Hobby Lobby Stores, Inc.*, 573 U.S. 682 (2014) |

RFRA's definition of "person" borrows the Dictionary Act. *Hobby Lobby* is the Court applying that to commercial entities. The original *Smith* plaintiffs were not corporations.

## Routing

Enabled: RFRA claim in federal court; exemption from the federal mandate. Closed: *Smith*-style application of a general rule to an individual practitioner; a secular firm's parallel objection.

State RFRA and RLUIPA ([pla-059](rluipa-property-tax-franchise.md)) fill the *Boerne* hole for land use and prisoners, not for every *Smith* fact.

## Application Evidence

*Smith* facts vs *Hobby Lobby* facts are the two-track evidence. RFRA was enacted with broad religious-coalition support after *Smith*; the 2014 application to for-profits is the commercial overlay the 1993 coalition did not have to name. Subsequent federal RFRA claims by businesses and ministries (immigration, health-care mandates, grants) are application; scope-label by case.

*Boerne* itself was a church-expansion permit fight — institutional land — which Congress then specialized in RLUIPA.
