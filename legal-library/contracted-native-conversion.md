# Contracted Native conversion

After the Civil War the United States assigned reservations and schools to Christian denominations (Grant's Peace Policy) and paid churches to run boarding schools. Children were taken from families. Language, dress, and ritual were interrupted. That is a legal barrier to **belief, expression, and receipt** — the original objects of religious liberty — administered as a federal contract. The analog is a settler parent keeping children and a church without a BIA contractor on the other side of the door.

Interior's Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative (Volume I, 2022; Volume II, 2024) is the government's own inventory. This page does not retry every school. It names the **designated religious contractor** as the preferential enabler.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is state-paid conversion capacity. The parties who obtain it are the denominations and orders that held contracts and the Interior/BIA offices that let them. Native children and parents take the residual: custody loss, language loss, and, in documented cases, death at school.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

First Amendment free exercise for settlers, including the churches that ran the schools. A public school that may not compose a prayer (*Engel*) while a contract school exists to replace a child's religion. The closed analog is leaving the child with the family and the community's own belief and speech.

**Other complex:** designated cost-plus contractor ([pla-048](designated-cost-plus-contractor.md)) — public money to a named shop. Funding-conditioned mandates ([pla-043](funding-conditioned-mandates.md)) — the purse as compulsion. Later faith-based funding ([pla-060](faith-based-public-money.md)) is the inverted purse: churches as social-service franchisees by "equal participation."

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Civilization Fund Act (1819) | Early federal money for "civilization," including missionary schools | 3 Stat. 516 (1819) |
| Grant Peace Policy | Reservations and agencies assigned to Protestant (and later other) denominations | Executive policy, 1869–; contract school system |
| Dawes-era allotment overlay | Land fractionation alongside school assimilation | General Allotment Act, 24 Stat. 388 (1887) |
| BIA / contract boarding schools | Federal custody and payment to church-run and federal schools | Interior/BIA contracting, 19th–20th c. |

Carlisle and its imitators are application, not a separate statute. The preferential design is the **contract**: the United States pays a church to hold the child.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative custody; contractual payment to religious operators; regulations that punish Native language and ritual at school. Closed: a Free Exercise claim by the child or parent that would stop the taking.

## Application Evidence

Interior Volume I (May 2022): from 1819 to 1969, **408** federal Indian boarding schools across 37 states or then-territories (21 in Alaska, 7 in Hawaii). Volume II (July 2024): official list updated to **417** institutions; **at least 973** American Indian, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian children died while attending federally operated or supported schools; at least 74 burial sites at 65 school sites; federal appropriations more than **$23.3 billion** in FY2023 inflation-adjusted dollars (1871–1969) for the system and associated assimilation policies. Scope: Interior's inventories, those years, "at least" death counts. Religious-institution participation is documented in Volume II; cite the report rather than a denomination-by-denomination library verdict.

Meriam Report (1928) is contemporaneous evidence that the system was already known to be harmful while it still ran.
