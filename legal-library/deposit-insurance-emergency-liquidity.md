# Deposit insurance and emergency liquidity facilities

In a run, the law does not treat all money-claims alike. Deposits at insured depository institutions are backed by FDIC insurance (and, in practice, by a political option to cover more). The same institutions have the discount window. In "unusual and exigent circumstances" the Board may authorize broad facilities under section 13(3). Uninsured cash, non-member paper, and money-market claims without a facility take the run. The 2023 Bank Term Funding Program is a recent illustration: term loans against Treasuries at par-related terms for depository institutions after SVB and Signature failed.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is survival and inward flight of funding toward designated banks. The parties who obtain it are insured depositories and, when 13(3) is opened, the eligible participants in that facility. Uninsured creditors of a failed bank, holders of cash outside the system, and institutions without window access take the delayed, run-end.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

An uninsured note or a bag of currency with no backstop; a nonbank that cannot discount at the window; a 19th-c. country bank at the bottom of the reserve pyramid ([pla-025](national-banking-reserve-pyramid.md)). Those analog claims shrink first in a panic. Insurance plus the window reverse the run **toward** the designated core.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| FDIC insurance | Statutory guarantee of deposits at insured institutions up to the limit (and systemic exceptions) | 12 U.S.C. ch. 16; § 1821 |
| Discount window | Standing advances to eligible depository institutions | FRA §§ 10B, 13; 12 U.S.C. § 347 |
| Section 13(3) | In unusual and exigent circumstances, Board may authorize Reserve Banks to discount for participants in a broad-based facility | [12 U.S.C. § 343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/343) |
| BTFP (2023) | One-year term credit to depositories against Treasuries, agency debt, and agency MBS; 13(3) plus Treasury approval | Fed term sheet, Mar. 12, 2023 |
| Treasury consultation | Post-Dodd-Frank 13(3) requires Treasury Secretary approval | 12 U.S.C. § 343(3) |

**First receiver in a panic:** the insured deposit and the window-eligible bank. **Delayed end:** the uninsured remainder, and every claim that is not wired to a facility.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative payout, window borrowing, emergency facilities after a Board finding and Treasury sign-off. Closed: equal treatment of all dollar claims in a run; a non-designated issuer getting the same backstop.

13(3) was added in 1932, little used until 2008, used again for broad facilities in 2020 and for BTFP in 2023. "Unusual and exigent" is the valve. It opens for a class, not for a household's cash box.

## Application Evidence

**Design.** FDIC insurance is available only at insured depository institutions. The discount window's eligibility page: depository institutions under FRA § 19(b)(1)(A) that maintain reservable accounts. That is a legal class.

**BTFP.** CRS IN12134: the Board found unusual and exigent circumstances under 12 U.S.C. § 343; Treasury approved. Collateral was Treasuries and agency paper — the same assets the first-receiver complex already holds ([pla-029](fiat-first-receiver-impulse.md)). Par-related lending against those assets is a privilege of the institutions that own them, not of the street economy that holds deposits inside those institutions only up to the insured cap (unless a systemic exception is declared).

Flight-to-safety into insured deposits and T-bills is the observed routing. The code makes that flight rational.
