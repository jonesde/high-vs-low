# Religious mass-tort bankruptcy

When abuse and similar claims threaten a religious entity's assets, the entity can file Chapter 11. The automatic stay stops ordinary trials. A plan channels claims into a trust. Insurers and parish property fights determine the pot. Claimants vote and take a percentage. Some states still retain charitable-immunity rules that cap or bar tort suits against charities, including churches. The analog is a non-religious mass-tort defendant that also uses Chapter 11 — **except** the religious debtor also has no 990 ([pla-052](church-tax-opacity.md)), autonomy objections to files ([pla-055](clergy-privilege-discovery-walls.md)), and a cultural presumption that the treasury is sacred.

This page does not decide the right dollar for any claimant. It names the **routing**: ordinary judgment vs plan.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a closed class and a fund. The parties who obtain it are the filing diocese, order, or similar entity, and often insurers. Claimants take whatever the plan and the remaining assets yield. Parishes and donors sometimes find that "church property" was never reachable.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A secular corporation in Chapter 11 for product or sexual-abuse torts (also a channeling tool — [pla-010](white-collar-civil-routing.md) adjacent). A 990-filing charity whose finances were public before the filing. An ordinary tort judgment with discovery and a jury. Charitable immunity, where it survives, is the older bar: no suit at all. The closed analog is a merits trial against the institution that employed or supervised the offender, with books in the open.

**Other complex:** PREP/CICP ([pla-042](covered-person-liability-shields.md)) — a limited public fund instead of a jury. Forfeiture ([pla-041](self-funding-forfeiture-enforcement.md)) extracts from the outside; this page protects the inside treasury.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Chapter 11 | Reorganization; automatic stay; plan; possible channeling | [11 U.S.C. §§ 1101 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1101) |
| Automatic stay | Stops ordinary trials and collection | 11 U.S.C. § 362 |
| Charitable immunity | State common-law or statutory caps/bars on charity torts (uneven, often narrowed) | State law |
| First Amendment / autonomy | Property characterization and discovery fights inside the case | [pla-049](church-autonomy-ecclesiastical-abstention.md), [pla-055](clergy-privilege-discovery-walls.md) |

Corporation sole and parish-vs-diocese title are the entity tricks that decide what is "property of the estate." Opacity of pre-petition books is [pla-052](church-tax-opacity.md).

## Routing

Enabled: bankruptcy stay; claims bar date; plan confirmation; immunity where it still exists. Closed: an unstayed jury trial against the institution with full discovery.

Non-religious mass-tort bankruptcies exist (Boy Scouts is a large mixed case: BSA is not a church but used similar youth-serving structure). The preferential overlay for **churches** is the combination of Chapter 11 with 990 silence, clergy privilege, and autonomy — not Chapter 11 alone.

## Application Evidence

Diocese of Harrisburg filed Chapter 11 in February 2020 after the Pennsylvania grand-jury wave; the petition described limited assets against large claim exposure. Other US dioceses and orders have filed in the 2000s–2020s (Portland, Spokane, San Diego, Milwaukee, and later waves after SOL windows). Scope-label each filing by court and date. Do not invent a national average cents-on-the-dollar.

Pennsylvania 2018 grand jury ([pla-055](clergy-privilege-discovery-walls.md)) is the fact pattern that made bankruptcy the residual court for several of those dioceses.
