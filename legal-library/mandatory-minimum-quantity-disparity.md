# Mandatory-minimum quantity disparity

The Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1986 wrote 100-to-1 quantity ratios into federal cocaine mandatory minimums: 5 grams of crack or 500 grams of powder for five years; 50 grams of crack or 5 kilograms of powder for ten. The 1988 Act added a five-year mandatory minimum for simple possession of 5 grams of crack. Pharmacologically the base is cocaine. The preferential split is **form plus typical user class**. Fair Sentencing Act of 2010 moved the ratio to 18-to-1; it did not equalize.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a longer federal sentence for a smaller mass of the same drug when the market is the crack market. The parties who obtain it are federal prosecutors with a mandatory floor that removes judicial mercy. Defendants in powder cases, and later Fair Sentencing beneficiaries, take the lighter statutory track. USSC and CRS have documented that crack cases disproportionately involved Black defendants.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Powder cocaine trafficking at 500 grams: same five-year floor as 5 grams of crack (1986). A physician-diverted oxycodone case on the medical track ([pla-044](licensing-monopoly-on-the-analog.md)). White-collar quantity fraud routed civil ([pla-010](white-collar-civil-routing.md)). The closed analog is treating weight of cocaine base as weight of cocaine base.

**Other complex:** procurement and defense-contractor fraud with loss tables that rarely produce the same floors as street drug weight. The MIC analog is loss calculated after negotiation; the street analog is a scale.

## Routing

Enabled: mandatory prison floors keyed to form and weight; charging selection by US Attorneys. Closed: sentencing as a judicial finding of comparable harm; equal grams, equal floor.

The 1986 Act is the named Drug War's charging engine. Nixon's 1971 label and DEA (1973) built the agency. 1986 wrote the quantity machine that hit street markets.

## Application Evidence

CRS IF11965: CSA as enacted did not distinguish powder and crack; 1986 created 100:1; 5 kg powder or 50 g crack = 10 years; 500 g powder or 5 g crack = 5 years. ACLU and USSC reporting: no pharmacological basis for 100:1; racial disparity in application. After 2010 the remaining 18:1 is still a statutory form split.

Scope: federal cocaine cases, not all state drug cases. Many states copied or later repealed similar ratios. Label the jurisdiction.

This page does not claim 1986 was enacted with a proven single motive. It claims the **code treats two forms of one drug as 100 (then 18) times different**, and that the heavier track was the poorer, more minority street market.
