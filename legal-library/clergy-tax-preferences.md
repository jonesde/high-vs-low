# Clergy tax preferences

26 U.S.C. § 107 lets a "minister of the gospel" exclude from gross income the rental value of a home furnished as pay, or a rental allowance used to rent or provide a home. Analog employees include employer-provided housing unless they fit the narrower convenience-of-the-employer lodging rule in § 119. Clergy also sit on special FICA/SECA tracks (self-employment tax with possible exemptions). The preferential enabler is a **status in the tax code**, not a showing that the housing is small or that the work is unpaid.

Prosperity-gospel housing and aircraft are the loud application. Do not invent assessed values. The Code does not scale the exclusion to poverty.

This page does not decide whether ministers should be paid. It names who is taxed on the house.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a lower effective tax on designated religious workers' housing (and related payroll treatment). The parties who obtain it are ministers who can claim § 107 and the churches that designate allowances. Analog teachers, nonprofit staff, and for-profit employees take the residual: tax on the same kind of housing.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

§ 119 lodging: on the business premises, required as a condition of employment, for the employer's convenience — a fact-intensive, limited exclusion. Ordinary W-2 housing allowances are income. The closed analog is a hospital chaplain or a secular nonprofit director with a housing stipend, taxed as wages, while a congregation's minister excludes the designated amount.

*Gaylor v. Mnuchin*, 919 F.3d 420 (7th Cir. 2019): the Seventh Circuit reversed a district court that had held § 107(2) unconstitutional under the Establishment Clause. The exclusion remaining in force is the preferential fact. The district court's contrary holding is the analog argument that lost on appeal.

| § 119 (contrast) | Narrow lodging exclusion for convenience of the employer | 26 U.S.C. § 119 |
| Clergy FICA/SECA | Ministers often treated as self-employed for Social Security; exemption path exists | 26 U.S.C. §§ 1402(e), 3121(b)(8) |
| *Gaylor* | § 107(2) held constitutional in the Seventh Circuit | *Gaylor v. Mnuchin*, 919 F.3d 420 (7th Cir. 2019) |

§ 107 dates to the 1921 housing exclusion for ministers; the cash-allowance prong is later statutory expansion. The franchise grew with suburban congregations and then with broadcast ministries.

## Routing

Enabled: church designation of a housing allowance; exclusion on the minister's return. Closed: treating the same dollars as wages for an analog employee.

IRS may challenge **amount** as unreasonable relative to housing. That is a valuation fight inside the franchise, not a repeal. Church opacity ([pla-052](church-tax-opacity.md)) makes public checks of large allowances harder.

## Application Evidence

The statute's text is the mechanism: "minister of the gospel" plus a furnished home or a rental allowance. Joint Committee on Taxation and IRS Publication 517 describe the exclusion as unique to clergy. Scope-label any dollar estimates of revenue cost by JCT year; do not invent a nationwide average allowance.

Designation of allowances that cover large residences is the prosperity-gospel application. Without 990s, the public evidence is often property records and aircraft registrations, not a church return.
