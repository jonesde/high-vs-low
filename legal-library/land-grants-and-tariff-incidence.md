# Public-domain grants and tariff incidence

Two prongs of the same game: **privilege to take the resource**, and **privilege to take a wedge on trade in resources**. Nineteenth-century railroad land grants moved public-domain acreage (often in checkerboard) to franchisees on a scale no homesteader could match. Tariff statutes move a price wedge to protected domestic first receivers before consumers and import-using producers pay. The analog is homestead-scale entry on the same domain, or a duty too small to reallocate real claims.

This is not a tariff-politics essay. It is two legally designated first-receiver slots.

## Outcome / Goal

**Grants.** Railroads and similar franchisees received millions of acres as subsidy for a line. Alternate sections created a checkerboard that raised the value of the remaining public (and railroad) sections. Homesteaders took 160-acre claims on what was left.

**Tariffs.** A statutory or delegated duty raises the domestic price of the protected good. Protected producers collect the higher price immediately. Households and downstream firms pay as the street price adjusts. Export-exposed sectors pay via retaliation or relative-price shifts. The first receiver is the protected sector (and, when the duty is collected, the Treasury). The delayed end is the CPI basket.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Homestead Act of 1862: 160 acres to an actual settler. A revenue tariff at a rate too small to protect. Free entry onto the public domain without a franchise grant. Those analogs were not how the transcontinental lines or the 2025–26 duty patchwork were built.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Pacific Railway Act 1862 (and 1864 enlargement) | Land grants in alternate sections along the route; bond subsidy | 12 Stat. 489; 13 Stat. 356 |
| Homestead Act 1862 (contrast) | 160-acre entry for settlers | 12 Stat. 392 |
| Section 232 | President may restrict imports that threaten to impair national security after a Commerce finding | [19 U.S.C. § 1862](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1862) |
| Section 301 | USTR may impose duties against unjustifiable or discriminatory foreign acts | [19 U.S.C. § 2411](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/2411) |
| IEEPA | Emergency regulation of transactions; used for 2025 country tariffs; Supreme Court held in Feb. 2026 it does not authorize those tariffs; IEEPA duties ended Feb. 24, 2026 | 50 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq.; CRS R48549 |
| Section 122 | Temporary 150-day surcharge; used Feb. 24, 2026 as a stopgap after IEEPA | 19 U.S.C. § 2132 |

**First receiver (grants):** the railroad (and its land-company affiliates). **First receiver (tariffs):** protected domestic producers at the new price; Treasury as to collected duty. **Delayed end:** homesteaders on leftover sections; consumers and import-using producers.

## Routing

Enabled: statutory grant of public land to a named class of franchisees; delegated tariff authorities (232, 301, and, while it lasted, IEEPA; then § 122). Closed: equal 160-acre access to the same corridor; a uniform low revenue tariff without a protective wedge.

Date 2025–26 actions. Do not personalize. CRS R48549 (Jan. 2026): second-term tariffs under IEEPA and § 232. Feb. 20, 2026: Supreme Court held IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs at issue; EO 14389 ended those IEEPA duties; § 232 and § 301 remain; § 122 used as a 150-day global surcharge from Feb. 24, 2026. That sequence is the live application of the **wedge**, not a completed policy.

## Application Evidence

**Grants.** The 1862/1864 Pacific acts are the citable transfer: public domain in alternate sections, plus bonds, to a franchisee charged with building a line. Checkerboard is the design that lets the franchisee capture appreciation on both granted and adjacent land. Homestead is the smaller, later analog on what remains.

**Tariffs.** Incidence is the evidence type, not a single "tariffs raise CPI by X" number unless sourced. A protective duty is a legally created price gap. Protected first receivers sell into that gap at once. Downstream prices and real wages move later — the same impulse/lag split as [pla-029](fiat-first-receiver-impulse.md), on goods rather than on new balances.

IEEPA's 2026 invalidation is evidence that the **choice of statute** matters. Congress named 232 and 301 as tariff machines; the Court held IEEPA was not. The administration then used the named machines and § 122. The first-receiver slot survived the caption change.
