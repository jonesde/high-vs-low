# Disfavored-practice criminalization

*Reynolds v. United States* (1879) held that the Free Exercise Clause does not protect polygamy: belief is free, the analog **practice** may be a crime. The Edmunds Act (1882) and Edmunds-Tucker Act (1887) went further: they dissolved the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints as a corporation and escheated property. *Employment Division v. Smith* (1990) held that a generally applicable criminal law (peyote) may deny unemployment benefits to a Native practitioner without a compelling-interest test. The analog is sacramental wine under Volstead, or a later RFRA "person" ([pla-058](rfra-commercial-overlay.md)).

This is the inversion spine of the wave. Individual belief and practice of the unfavored are closed. Institutional claimants later get a statute. Pair *Reynolds* (polygamy crushed, property taken) with the later LDS institutional franchise on tax, land, and politics: a **routing change**, not a theology score.

This page does not decide whether any practice was good. It names who was allowed to do the analog.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is selective criminalization of religious practice. The parties who obtain it are the federal and state governments that write the generally applicable crime, and later the organized claimants who win RFRA/RLUIPA. Unfavored practitioners take the criminal or benefit-denial track.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Sacramental exceptions under the Volstead Act ([pla-037](constitutional-purity-prohibition.md)). *Sherbert* / *Yoder*-era individual exemptions, which *Smith* narrowed. Closely held corporations under *Hobby Lobby*. The closed analog is the unfavored person's practice treated as belief-plus-conduct that a court must accommodate.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** *Smith* (individual Native, peyote, unemployment) loses. *Hobby Lobby* (closely held for-profit, RFRA "person") wins. Same Free Exercise vocabulary; different claimant class.

**Other complex:** CSA scheduling ([pla-038](tax-schedule-as-crime.md)) — administrative crime for the analog molecule; licensed track for the franchisee.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| *Reynolds* | Practice of polygamy is not Free Exercise; belief/practice split | *Reynolds v. United States*, 98 U.S. 145 (1879) |
| Edmunds Act | Polygamy as federal crime in the territories; disqualification | Edmunds Act, 22 Stat. 30 (1882) |
| Edmunds-Tucker | Dissolve LDS corporation; escheat property to the United States | Edmunds-Tucker Act, 24 Stat. 635 (1887) |
| *Smith* | Neutral, generally applicable criminal law needs no compelling-interest test | *Employment Division v. Smith*, 494 U.S. 872 (1990) |

*Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association*, 485 U.S. 439 (1988), is the land analog: road through sacred ground is not a Free Exercise violation. Native belief and receipt lose; later RLUIPA ([pla-059](rluipa-property-tax-franchise.md)) is the institutional land franchise.

## Routing

Enabled: generally applicable crime; benefit denial; territorial corporation-killing. Closed: a merits accommodation for the unfavored practitioner's analog ritual.

RFRA was Congress's answer to *Smith*. It did not restore *Reynolds*'s victims. It created a new claimant class ([pla-058](rfra-commercial-overlay.md)).

## Application Evidence

Edmunds-Tucker did not merely criminalize a marriage form. It **took the church as property**. That is institution-killing for the unfavored, the inverse of autonomy ([pla-049](church-autonomy-ecclesiastical-abstention.md)) for the favored.

*Smith*'s facts: two members of the Native American Church, fired and denied unemployment for sacramental peyote. The Court chose the generally applicable crime. Native Church peyote later received a statutory exemption (American Indian Religious Freedom Act Amendments of 1994). Scope: that statute, that practice — not a general restoration of *Sherbert*.
