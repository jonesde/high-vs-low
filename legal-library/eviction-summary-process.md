# Eviction summary process

A landlord who wants the unit back uses **unlawful detainer** or **summary process**: a specialized civil docket measured in days or weeks, not ordinary civil years. A tenant who stays without paying, or a landlord who changes the locks without the writ, takes a crime or a slower suit. The analog is treating possession of a home like any other civil controversy. Pair with [pla-008](secured-credit-foreclosure.md): the note recovers the house through foreclosure; the lease recovers it through eviction. Federal overlays (Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act; CARES Act 30-day notice) are **temporary or narrow** cracks, not the default.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is rapid restoration of possession to the owner of the leasehold or the building. The parties who obtain it are landlords who can file and serve. Occupants take the residual: a short answer period, a default judgment, and a lockout.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Ordinary civil litigation timing for the same dollars. Self-help by the landlord (crime in many states). The closed analog is a merits trial on the rent, the repair, or the title at the same speed as any other contract case — or a tenant remaining without a summary writ.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** Housing is "home" in political speech and a **summary chattel** on the docket. Source-of-income ordinances (vouchers) in some cities are a partial analog opening; they are local, not the national default.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Unlawful detainer / summary process | Fast civil possession action | State codes (e.g. Cal. Civ. Proc. Code §§ 1159–1179a; N.Y. RPAPL Art. 7) |
| Forcible entry / self-help bans | Landlord who skips the writ commits a crime or a tort | State penal and civil codes |
| Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act | Notice to bona fide tenants after foreclosure; expired 2014, restored 2018 | Pub. L. 111-22 (2009); later restoration — date-label |
| CARES Act 30-day notice | Temporary extra notice on federally backed properties | Pub. L. 116-136 (2020) — **temporary** overlay |
| CDC eviction moratorium | Administrative pause, then *Alabama Ass'n of Realtors v. HHS* (2021) | Temporary; not the default docket |

## Routing

Enabled: summary civil possession; lockout after judgment. Closed: ordinary civil calendar; or remaining in the unit without a writ.

## Application Evidence

Princeton Eviction Lab: in a typical pre-pandemic year, landlords filed about **3.6 million** eviction cases nationally (Lab's national estimate; 2018 filings against 2.7 million unique households). The Lab's tracking subset (not a full national census) recorded on the order of **1.2 million** filings in the sites it monitors in 2024–2025. Scope-label Lab year and geography. Do not invent a national plaintiff win-rate.

The docket speed is the preferential design: possession is the remedy, rent the residual. Habitability counterclaims ([pla-077](habitability-paper-nonenforcement.md)) exist in many states and still sit **inside** the summary case.
