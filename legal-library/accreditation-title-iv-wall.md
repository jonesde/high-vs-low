# Accreditation and Title IV wall

The Higher Education Act of 1965, Title IV, 20 U.S.C. § 1070 et seq., makes federal student aid (grants, loans, work-study) available to students at institutions that hold **recognized accreditation** (and other eligibility). The Secretary of Education recognizes accrediting agencies; those agencies are private actors with a public purse. State occupational licenses often require a degree from an accredited school. The analog is an equipped teacher or shop that has the books, the lab, and the skill without the stamp. This is license-as-wall ([pla-044](licensing-monopoly-on-the-analog.md), [pla-016](technical-data-export-controls.md)) applied to instruction.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is that federal aid and many licenses run only through the accredited franchise. The parties who obtain it are recognized accreditors, eligible institutions, and the loan servicers on Title IV paper. Unaccredited schools and their students take the residual: cash pay, no aid, and often no license.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

An equipped instructor or independent school whose students can sit for the same license and borrow on the same terms. The closed analog is treating demonstrated competence as the gate, not the stamp. "National" vs "regional" vs programmatic accreditors are fights **inside** the franchise.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** Accreditation is sold as quality. Title IV eligibility is the economic fact. Gainful-employment and borrower-defense fights police **which** accredited shops keep the aid, not whether the analog shop may have it.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative eligibility plus contractual participation agreement. Closed: analog school as of right for aid and license.

## Application Evidence

Title IV's accreditation condition is the mechanism. Department of Education recognition lists are the live franchise roster. Loss of accreditation (or of recognition) is the death of the aid stream — a routing event, not a verdict on whether the teachers could teach.

State bar, nursing, and teaching-credential rules that require a degree from an accredited institution are the labor overlay. Equipped graduates of unaccredited programs who cannot sit are the analog closed class.
