# Gold withdrawal and the 1934 redefinition of the dollar

In 1933 the United States forbade "hoarding" of gold coin, bullion, and gold certificates and required delivery to the banks and then to the Treasury. The Gold Reserve Act of 1934 vested title in the United States and changed the statutory gold content of the dollar from $20.67 to $35 an ounce. Holders of coin took a legally set price. The issue privilege, previously constrained by a 40% gold backing of Fed notes, expanded. First receiver of the new room to issue: the Treasury and the Reserve Banks. Delayed end: anyone who had treated the old gold dollar as the unit of their savings or contracts.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is transfer of the monetary metal from private holders to the United States, plus a cheaper gold definition that lets more notes stand against the same stock. The parties who obtain it are the Treasury (including the Exchange Stabilization Fund funded by the revaluation profit) and the Federal Reserve. Private holders of coin and gold-clause creditors take the residual.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Redeeming a Federal Reserve note for gold coin at $20.67 per ounce, as the 1913 Act's framework contemplated. Holding coin as lawful money outside the banking system. Those analogs were closed by emergency statute, executive order, and the 1934 Act.

**First receiver:** Treasury and Reserve Banks, which obtain the metal and the seigniorage from redefinition. **Delayed end:** households ordered to deliver coin at the old statutory price while the new official price is $35; gold-clause creditors paid in devalued dollars.

## Routing

Enabled: criminal/civil penalties for hoarding; administrative call-in; statutory redefinition. Closed: convertibility at the old parity; private gold as circulating money.

The 1913 Act required gold equal to 40% of Fed notes. By the early 1930s that ratio was the binding constraint. Calling in gold and then marking it up to $35 is the legal sequence that relaxes the constraint for the issuer, not for the prior holder.

## Application Evidence

Fed History ("Roosevelt's Gold Program"): the 1913 gold standard was built into the Fed; 40% gold against notes at $20.67. EO 6102 and the Gold Reserve Act are the dated instruments. The revaluation profit funded the Exchange Stabilization Fund — a first-receiver vehicle for the increment.

This page does not argue 1933 macro policy. It names who was legally required to surrender the metal and who received title and the redefinition gain.

Private gold ownership was later liberalized (1970s). The 1934 redefinition and the end of domestic convertibility are what remain as the break in the unit.
