# Tax stamp and administrative schedule as crime

The Harrison Narcotics Tax Act (1914) used registration and a special tax to control opium and coca. Courts read failure to comply as a crime, including for physicians who prescribed maintenance. The Controlled Substances Act (1970) dropped the tax costume and let the Attorney General place substances on five schedules. Schedule I means no accepted medical use in DEA's view and a trafficking/possession crime. The Analog Act (1986) treats a "substantially similar" unlisted molecule as Schedule I if intended for human consumption. The analog for the street user is the same molecule on a DEA-registered prescription or an FDA-approved product.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is administrative criminalization. The parties who obtain it are DEA, FDA (medical-use findings), and registered manufacturers and prescribers. Unregistered possessors and analog chemists take the criminal track. Congress does not need a new amendment for each substance ([pla-037](constitutional-purity-prohibition.md) needed one for alcohol).

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A doctor with a DEA number writing oxycodone (Schedule II). A pharmacy filling it. A company with FDA approval. The closed analog is possession of heroin, street fentanyl, or an unscheduled analog intended for consumption. Pharmacology is not the split; **registration and schedule** are.

**Other complex:** ITAR/EAR commodity jurisdiction ([pla-016](technical-data-export-controls.md)) — an administrative list that makes ordinary technical transfer a crime. Export classification is the MIC/intel parallel of CSA scheduling.

| Scheduling factors | Eight factors including medical use and abuse potential; hearing "on the record" | 21 U.S.C. § 811(c) |
| Analog Act | Unlisted analog of SI/SII treated as SI if for human consumption | 21 U.S.C. § 813 (1986) |
| DEA (1973) | Successor to BNDD/FBN as the scheduling-and-enforcement agency | Reorg. Plan No. 2 of 1973 |

Nixon's June 1971 "war on drugs" speech and the 1973 DEA reorganization are the policy labels. The CSA is the code.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative placement on a schedule, then criminal prosecution. Closed: a jury trial on whether *this* molecule is a public-order harm before it is a crime; a tax-only civil track like a liquor license after 1933.

Schedule I's "no currently accepted medical use" is an agency conclusion that bars research and licensed medical use at once. That is the purity lock without an amendment.

## Application Evidence

CRS R45948: CSA is the unified federal framework; powder and crack were the same CSA substance until 1986 quantity triggers ([pla-039](mandatory-minimum-quantity-disparity.md)). Analog provision reaches designer molecules without new legislation.

Harrison as applied to physicians (*Webb*, *Jin Fuey Moy* era) is the early split: the registered class may handle; maintenance of an addict was read as not legitimate practice. The medical analog was closed for the poor patient and left open as "practice" for the profession's core.

Cannabis remaining Schedule I for decades while states licensed medical/adult use is application evidence of administrative crime outrunning local analog tracks.
