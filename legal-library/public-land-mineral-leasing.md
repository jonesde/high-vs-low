# Exclusive federal mineral leases

The Mineral Leasing Act of 1920 pulled oil, gas, coal, oil shale, phosphate, and sodium off the 1872 claim-patent system and onto leases. The United States keeps title. A lessee gets the exclusive right to extract, against a royalty and rent. Outer Continental Shelf oil and gas sit on a parallel exclusive-lease track (OCSLA). The analog is unleased federal mineral remaining open, or non-exclusive public use. The preferential fact is **who holds the exclusive extraction slot**, not whether a royalty exists.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a time-limited exclusive franchise over a public mineral. The parties who obtain it are the high bidders and the firms that can staff BLM/BOEM process, post bonds, and drill. The residual public gets royalty and rent if the lease produces, and nothing from acreage held off production. Unleased land is not a commons anyone may tap.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Hardrock location without a royalty ([pla-032](hardrock-mining-patent-1872.md)). A competitive lease is more "public residual" than a $5 patent, and still an exclusive right against other equipped extractors. Homestead or grazing without the mineral estate.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Mineral Leasing Act of 1920 | Lease of oil, gas, coal, and other named minerals on public domain land; royalty and rent | 30 U.S.C. § 181 et seq. |
| OCSLA | Federal lease of Outer Continental Shelf oil and gas | 43 U.S.C. § 1331 et seq. |
| BLM / BOEM process | Competitive sale, stipulations, permits to drill | Agency regulations |
| Contrast: 1872 location | Locatable hardrock remains outside this lease system | 30 U.S.C. ch. 2 |

**First receiver:** the lessee with exclusive extraction rights (and, when prices jump after lease issuance, the party already on the acreage). **Delayed end:** later bidders; the residual public as to unproduced held acreage; surface users who do not own the mineral.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative lease plus exclusive right to produce. Closed: a second equipped firm drilling the same federal mineral without a lease; treating unleased federal oil as a commons.

Date leasing and permitting waves as application evidence. Do not write an administration as the villain. The 1920 Act is the standing machine; later EOs and five-year OCS programs change volume, not the exclusive-slot design.

## Application Evidence

BLM: the 1920 Act "provided for the leasing of minerals from public lands including oil, gas, coal and other non-energy leasable minerals." CRS: MLA "removed oil, gas, oil shale, phosphates, sodium, and other minerals from the Mining Law claim-patent system."

The royalty is the public residual. The exclusive lease is the interposition. A firm that can bid, litigate NEPA, and post a bond occupies the slot. A local user of the same land does not.

Hold-by-production and lease speculation are application patterns: exclusive rights can sit between the public and the mineral **without** extraction. That is closer to [pla-020](blocking-patents-nonuse.md) than to a working commons.
