# Covered-person liability shields

The Public Readiness and Emergency Preparedness Act (2005) lets the HHS Secretary declare that "covered persons" are immune from suit and liability under federal and state law for losses from "covered countermeasures." Emergency Use Authorization lets FDA permit a product that has not completed ordinary approval. Together they invert product liability: the maker and the administrator are the protected class; the recipient's analog suit is closed. COVID-19 is the large application (declaration March 2020; EUA vaccines and other countermeasures). This page does not decide whether SARS-CoV-2 originated in a lab or whether any product "worked." It names who may be sued.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a liability vacuum around a designated product and its chain. The parties who obtain it are manufacturers, distributors, program planners, and qualified persons who administer the countermeasure. Injured recipients are pointed to the Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program (CICP), a no-fault administrative fund with a high bar and no jury. Willful misconduct is a narrow residual path.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

An ordinary prescription drug: FDA approval, learned-intermediary warnings, state product-liability and failure-to-warn suits. The National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act (1986) already moved childhood vaccines to a special court; PREP is broader and emergency-triggered. The closed analog is suing Pfizer, Moderna, a hospital, or a pharmacist in tort for a covered COVID countermeasure the way one sues over a defective appliance.

**Other complex:** government-contractor immunity and the FTCA discretionary-function exception in the military-industrial complex. Designated makers of a "countermeasure" or a weapons system share the same shape: the inside is immune; the outside has a limited fund or nothing.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| PREP Act immunity | Covered persons immune for losses caused by or relating to administration or use of a covered countermeasure once the Secretary declares | [42 U.S.C. § 247d-6d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/247d-6d) |
| CICP | Administrative compensation; not a jury trial | 42 U.S.C. § 247d-6e |
| EUA | FDA may authorize unapproved products in an emergency | [21 U.S.C. § 360bbb-3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360bbb-3) |
| COVID PREP declaration | Secretary Azar, March 2020, medical countermeasures against COVID-19 | 85 Fed. Reg. 15198 (Mar. 17, 2020) |
| Vaccine Act (contrast) | Childhood-vaccine special court; still more process than CICP | 42 U.S.C. §§ 300aa-1 et seq. |

EUA products are not the same legal object as fully licensed products. Recipients were often told "vaccine" in ordinary speech. The statute said "covered countermeasure" and "emergency use." That gap is a preferential speech-and-liability stack, not a pharmacology holding.

## Routing

Enabled: Secretary declaration; immunity; CICP. Closed: state juries, punitive damages, discovery into manufacturing for covered claims.

Mandates ([pla-043](funding-conditioned-mandates.md)) plus this shield is the full pair: the outside is compelled to take the product; the inside cannot be sued in the ordinary way.

## Application Evidence

PREP's text: a covered person "shall be immune from suit and liability under Federal and State law" for qualifying losses. The March 2020 declaration triggered that for COVID countermeasures. CICP payment rates and denial rates (HRSA data, when cited) are the residual "court." Scope-label any CICP statistic by year.

NCVIA 1986 is the earlier health-complex shield. PREP is the emergency generalization. Both exist because the designated industry would not sit on ordinary product risk at the scale the program wanted.
