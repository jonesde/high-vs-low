# Exclusive private control of publicly funded technology

Federal funds pay for the invention. Bayh-Dole lets the contractor take title and exclusive-license it. Other firms with labs and capital may not practice the publicly funded technique without that license. March-in (35 U.S.C. § 203) exists on paper. CRS and Stanford reviews find **no federal agency has ever exercised it** in more than forty years.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is exclusive private control of taxpayer-funded inventions. The parties who obtain it are universities, contractors, and their exclusive licensees. Competing equipped firms, and the public that paid, get the residual: a patent they may not practice, plus a march-in petition that does not issue.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Works of the U.S. government itself are not copyrightable (17 U.S.C. § 105). That is the public-domain analog for copyright. For patents, a non-exclusive license, or title remaining in the government, would leave other equipped firms free to practice. Bayh-Dole flipped the default the other way (1980): contractor title, with government-use and march-in reservations that rarely move.

This is not drug unaffordability as such. It is exclusive rights over a technique the public already bought, blocking other parties who have the resources to make it.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Contractor title in subject inventions | Nonprofit and small-business contractors (and, by later expansion and executive order, others) may elect title | [35 U.S.C. § 202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/202) |
| Exclusive licensing | Contractor/licensee can exclude other equipped practitioners | 35 U.S.C. §§ 202–204 |
| March-in | Agency may compel licensing if the invention is not practically applied, or for health/safety/public-use/preference-for-US-industry grounds | [35 U.S.C. § 203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/203) |
| Government use | United States retains a paid-up license to practice | § 202(c)(4) |
| Contrast: US government works | No copyright in federal government works | [17 U.S.C. § 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105) |

March-in triggers in § 203(a) include failure to achieve practical application, health or safety needs, public-use requirements, and US manufacturing preference. Pricing-as-march-in remains contested (NIST 2023 draft guidance treated price as a consideration; no completed march-in on that or any other ground).

## Routing

Enabled: contractor title plus exclusive license plus ordinary patent infringement against outsiders. Closed: agency march-in as a working compulsory license. Also closed, as a default: public-domain or non-exclusive practice by other US firms.

Procedural features:

- March-in is initiated by the funding agency, not by a competitor as of right.
- Petitions (including NIH petitions on drugs) have been denied.
- Government-use rights under § 202(c)(4) and 28 U.S.C. § 1498 are a different, also rarely used, safety valve.

## Application Evidence

**Zero march-ins.** CRS (IF12582, 2024): "In the more than 40 years since Bayh-Dole's enactment, no federal agency has ever exercised its march-in rights." Stanford Law's 2024 feasibility review is to the same effect. The safety valve is structural non-use.

The preferential pattern is the default exclusive path vs. the unused compulsory path. Other equipped manufacturers remain infringers unless they take a sublicense.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 1 (Pride vs Humility and Equality).** Public payment does not yield public practice. The exclusive licensee is more equal as to the technique.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** March-in text looks like a limit. Non-use of march-in is the operating law.
- **Rule 33 (Dominion: stewardship or ownership).** Stewardship of a public investment becomes private ownership of the technique.
- **Rule 40 (Safeguards: principled or procedural).** § 203 is a procedural safeguard that does not fire.
- **Rule 42 (Production: distributed or centralized).** Other equipped firms are distributed capacity. Exclusive license centralizes who may produce.
