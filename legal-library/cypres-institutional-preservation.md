# Cy-pres institutional preservation

If a private trust's purpose fails, the analog is a resulting trust: assets go back to the settlor's estate. Charitable trusts get **cy-pres** ("as near as possible"): a court (or, under UPMIFA, an institution with notice to the attorney general) rewrites the purpose so the corpus stays in charitable hands. Administrative deviation adjusts means. The people named or implied in the original gift are not restored. The institution is.

Controllers: trustees, charity boards, and the AG who consents or does not object ([pla-064](ag-only-charitable-standing.md)). The claimed beneficiaries have no necessary seat.

This page does not decide whether any rewrite was "close enough." It names who keeps the assets.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is perpetual charitable title. The parties who obtain it are successor charities and trustees. Heirs and living analog beneficiaries take the residual: the gift does not come back.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A private spendthrift trust that fails. A gift over to named people. A spend-down charity. The closed analog is returning failed-purpose property to the family or to the living poor the settlor actually had in mind.

Class-action cy-pres (FRCP 23 residual funds paid to a charity the class did not choose) is a later overlay: the same rewrite tool, different caption. Short; not this page's core.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Common-law cy-pres | Rewrite purpose when original is impossible, impracticable, or (in many states) wasteful | State trust cases; Restatement (Third) of Trusts |
| UTC modification | Codified cy-pres and deviation | Uniform Trust Code §§ 413–414 as enacted |
| UPMIFA modification | Institution may modify a restriction with AG notice / court | UPMIFA § 6 (2006) |
| FRCP 23 residue (overlay) | Unclaimed class funds to a "next best" charity | Fed. R. Civ. P. 23; state analogs |

General charitable intent is the finding that unlocks cy-pres. Courts often infer it. That inference is the preferential design: keep it in the sector.

## Routing

Enabled: petition for cy-pres; UPMIFA notice-and-modify; AG non-objection. Closed: resulting trust to heirs; a right in analog beneficiaries to take the failed gift.

## Application Evidence

The doctrine's text is the mechanism: private trusts result; charitable trusts are rewritten. State cy-pres statutes after UTC adoption are the modern costume. UPMIFA's prefatory materials treat the AG as protector of both donor intent and the **public's interest in charitable funds** — i.e., the sector's interest in keeping the money.

Named university and hospital rewrites of "impracticable" scholarships or wards are illustrations; label each case. Do not invent a national rewrite count.
