# Secured credit, foreclosure, and priority recovery

A perfected security interest is a private lien the state will enforce ahead of almost everyone else. Institutional lenders recover from collateral, often without a full merits fight over the underlying relationship. Unsecured creditors, workers with wage claims that are not priority-capped, and individual debtors recover slower, recover less, and face garnishment, levies, and credit reporting that institutions can structure around.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is retention of value by secured creditors: repossession and sale of personal property under UCC Article 9, foreclosure of real property, deficiency judgments, and bankruptcy distributions that pay secured claims up to collateral value before general unsecured claims. The parties who obtain it are banks, finance companies, mortgagees, and other institutional secured parties. Unsecured trade creditors, tort claimants, and most consumer counterparties take the residual.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

An unsecured lender or a small supplier has a contract claim and, after judgment, whatever collection tools remain against unencumbered assets. In bankruptcy that claim is often paid cents on the dollar or discharged. An individual debtor faces wage garnishment, bank levies, and credit-reporting consequences that a well-advised entity debtor can often delay, stay, or restructure. The closed analog is not "unsecured credit is illegal"; it is that the fast, high-recovery path is legally assigned to parties who take and perfect collateral, a step that requires paper, filing systems, and counsel.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Attachment and enforceability | Security interest becomes enforceable against the debtor | UCC § 9-203 |
| Perfection and priority | Filing or possession puts the secured party ahead of later lien creditors and unsecured creditors | UCC §§ 9-308–9-339, 9-317 |
| Default remedies | Self-help repossession if no breach of the peace; disposition; deficiency | UCC §§ 9-609, 9-610, 9-615, 9-626 |
| Mortgage / deed of trust foreclosure | Judicial or nonjudicial sale of real property | State foreclosure statutes |
| Deficiency judgments | Personal liability after collateral sale, where not barred | State law |
| Bankruptcy secured claims | Allowed secured claim up to collateral value; stay relief to foreclose | [11 U.S.C. § 506](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/506); §§ 362, 363 |
| Priority unsecured crumbs | Limited wage and consumer-deposit priorities; the rest is general unsecured | 11 U.S.C. § 507 |

## Routing

Enabled: contractual security plus civil (and sometimes self-help) enforcement, preserved in bankruptcy. Closed: equal participation in the debtor's value by unsecured claimants, and cheap resistance by individual debtors.

Procedural features:

- Self-help repossession of personal property without a pre-deprivation hearing, limited by the breach-of-peace rule.
- Nonjudicial foreclosure in many states.
- Automatic stay pauses collection but does not erase priority; relief from stay is a standard secured-creditor motion.
- Credit reporting, garnishment, and bank-levy systems are built for collecting from natural persons. Entity debtors use different boxes (Chapter 11, forbearance, intercreditor agreements).

## Application Evidence

This entry is primarily structural. The code is the evidence. Do not invent a single national "secured recovery rate."

What can be stated without a fake percentage:

- A perfected secured party is senior to unsecured creditors as to the collateral (UCC § 9-201, § 9-317). Unsecured creditors can lose priority to a later secured party.
- In bankruptcy, the secured claim is paid from the collateral's value; the deficiency falls into general unsecured. General unsecured classes in consumer and many business cases recover little or nothing.
- Individual debtors are the usual targets of wage garnishment and consumer collection systems. Institutional creditors are the usual holders of perfected Article 9 and mortgage positions.

The preferential pattern is who can occupy the secured box: parties with standardized documents, filing operations, and the ability to monitor defaults. Small claimants (including many workers as unsecured wage creditors beyond the § 507 cap) cannot cost-effectively take the same position against a consumer or a failing firm.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 1 (Pride vs Humility and Equality).** Priority is a legal ranking of persons' claims. The secured party is "more equal" as to the asset.
- **Rule 15 (Response to loss: retake or expand).** Deficiency judgments and continuing collection expand beyond the collateral. Self-help plus deficiency is more than restoration of the unpaid loan from the pledged thing.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** Perfection and priority are formal. Missing a filing statement can erase a lifetime of "true" credit. The container is the right.
- **Rule 33 (Dominion: stewardship or ownership).** Collateral is treated as the creditor's to take, not as a relationship to restructure first.
- **Rule 42 (Production: distributed or centralized).** Access to capital is centralized in parties who can run Article 9 and mortgage machinery. Household and small-firm production sits downstream of that stack.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
