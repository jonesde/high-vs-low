# Federal Reserve Act 1913, elastic currency, and the discount window

The Act of December 23, 1913 created regional Reserve Banks "to furnish an elastic currency" and "to afford means of rediscounting commercial paper." Member banks — national banks mandatorily, state banks by election — became the legally designated first receivers of that elasticity. Federal Reserve notes are obligations of the United States, receivable for taxes. Non-members and the wage economy meet the notes later, after they have already been advanced against member-bank paper.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a standing issue privilege: a member bank can discount eligible paper at the window and receive Reserve notes or reserve balances. The parties who obtain it are member banks and, through them, their borrowers and the Treasury (government deposits, later open-market counterparties). Households and non-member institutions obtain the notes as they circulate, at the delayed street end.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

National-bank notes under the 1863–64 acts, backed by a frozen bond stock and gold reserve ratios, without a central discount window. A non-member bank that could not use a member as agent (the original Act forbade members from passing discounts for non-members). Private clearinghouse certificates as an extra-legal substitute ([pla-025](national-banking-reserve-pyramid.md)).

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Elastic currency and rediscount | Stated purposes of the Act | Preamble; signed Dec. 23, 1913 |
| Membership | National banks must join; state banks may; "member bank" defined | FRA §§ 1, 2, 9 |
| Discount for members | Reserve Banks discount notes, drafts, bills arising from actual commercial transactions | FRA § 13; [12 U.S.C. § 343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/343) (as later recodified) |
| Advances to members | Window lending against eligible paper / collateral | FRA §§ 10B, 13; 12 U.S.C. § 347 |
| Federal Reserve notes | Issued at Board discretion to Reserve Banks; obligations of the United States; receivable for taxes, customs, public dues | FRA § 16 |
| Original gold backing | 40% gold against notes, 35% gold or lawful money against deposits (later stripped) | FRA § 16 as enacted |
| Open-market operations | Reserve Banks buy and sell government and certain other obligations | FRA § 14; [12 U.S.C. § 355](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/355) |

**First receiver:** the member bank at the discount window, and the Reserve Bank that issues notes against that paper. **Delayed end:** the public that receives notes only after they have been advanced; 1913 gold-redemption language later becomes inoperative ([pla-028](gold-withdrawal-1933.md), [pla-029](fiat-first-receiver-impulse.md)).

## Routing

Enabled: membership plus discount plus note issue as a public obligation. Closed: unprivileged issue of a circulating ticket by a non-member; remaining on the inelastic national-bank-note track.

National banks that failed to join lost "rights, privileges, and franchises" under the original Act. Discounting for a non-member through a member as agent was forbidden. The window is not a public utility; it is a member facility.

## Application Evidence

The statute is the evidence. Section 16: notes "issued at the discretion of the Board ... for the purpose of making advances to Federal reserve banks ... and for no other purpose," "obligations of the United States," "receivable by all national and member banks ... and for all taxes, customs, and other public dues."

NY Fed history: member banks "became entitled to have access to discounted loans at the discount window." That entitlement is the first-receiver slot. Elasticity was the advertised fix for 1907; the legal design still runs new notes through the same class of institutions that sat at the top of the national-bank pyramid.

Do not treat the 6% dividend on Reserve Bank stock as the main prize. The prize is access to issue and to the window.
