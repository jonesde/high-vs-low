# Born-secret Restricted Data

The Atomic Energy Act defines "Restricted Data" as a **category of information**, not as a stamp the government puts on a document it created. Data concerning design, manufacture, or utilization of atomic weapons is Restricted Data unless formally declassified. A person who independently derives it from public sources still may not communicate it. That is a ban on **awareness-sharing** despite knowledge and resources. The analog is ordinary scientific publication.

*United States v. Progressive* (1979) is the one court test. It is inconclusive as precedent (preliminary injunction, then dismissed as moot). It is conclusive as routing: the government used the Act to restrain a magazine article compiled from unclassified sources.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is prior restraint and criminal exposure for communicating a nuclear-weapons technique, including one the speaker built from open literature. The party that obtains it is the United States (DOE/NNSA classification apparatus). Independent physicists, journalists, and later anyone whose model or article reconstitutes Restricted Data are the closed class.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Publishing an independently derived result in chemistry or cryptography is ordinary science (crypto still hits export rules; see [pla-016](technical-data-export-controls.md)). Restricted Data does not require a classification guide applied to a government document. It is "born secret." Declassification under 42 U.S.C. § 2162 is the only exit. Invention secrecy (35 U.S.C. §§ 181–188) is a related but distinct gag on patent applications.

| *Progressive* | Preliminary injunction against publication of an H-bomb article compiled from unclassified sources | *United States v. Progressive, Inc.*, 467 F. Supp. 990 (W.D. Wis. 1979) |

The definition is the confession: "all data" in the category, not "data we marked." Independently generated information in the category is still Restricted Data until declassified.

## Routing

Enabled: criminal statutes plus injunctive prior restraint. Closed: First Amendment publication of independently derived science in this category, pending declassification.

Procedural features:

- No need to prove the author stole a classified document.
- Declassification is an agency act, not a judicial finding that the information is public.
- *Progressive* never produced a Supreme Court holding; the government dropped the case after other publications. The injunction issued.

## Application Evidence

**Doctrine.** The born-secret reading is the standard account of § 2014(y): DOE does not "classify" Restricted Data so much as recognize a statutory category. Wikipedia and DOE historical writing match the statutory text.

**Illustration.** Howard Morland's article for *The Progressive*, "The H-Bomb Secret," was written from interviews and public sources. The United States sued under the Atomic Energy Act. The district court granted a preliminary injunction, with stated unease. Other outlets then published similar material; the case was dismissed as moot. There is no final appellate holding that the Act is constitutional as prior restraint. There is a documented use of the Act to stop a fully resourced magazine from printing independently assembled technique.

This page does not argue for publication of weapons design. It documents a legal category in which **having the knowledge is not permission to tell it**.
