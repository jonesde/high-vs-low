# Housing price-to-wage wedge after 1971

The analog person needs a roof. After the United States closed the gold window in 1971 ([pla-028](gold-withdrawal-1933.md), [pla-029](fiat-first-receiver-impulse.md)), new purchasing power hit dealers, banks, and Treasury counterparties first. Existing houses are the large household asset that re-prices. Mortgage-interest deduction (§ 163(h)), principal-residence gain exclusion (§ 121), and GSE charters (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac) sit on that inflation as tax and credit franchises for owners. The analog wage-earner cannot buy the same roof at the old multiple of pay. This page does not claim one statute "caused" prices. The preferential fact is **first-receiver asset inflation versus wage lag**, with zoning and credit as co-walls ([pla-074](exclusionary-zoning-housing-wall.md), [pla-008](secured-credit-foreclosure.md)).

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is that owners and leveraged first-receivers keep a larger house-price multiple; typical wages do not. The parties who obtain it are existing owners, mortgage originators, and GSE counterparties. Would-be first-time buyers take the residual: a higher price-to-income ratio.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

The same median roof at an early-1970s price-to-income multiple. A wage that re-prices with the house. The closed analog is new money appearing as wages, or a roof whose title is not the first-receiver's residual claim.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** Homeownership is sold as the analog person's capital. The tax expenditures (mortgage interest, gain exclusion) and the GSE bid support **incumbent owners and the credit desk**, not the person still renting.

## Routing

Enabled: owner deduction and gain exclusion; GSE purchase of the note; house as collateral that inflates. Closed: the analog wage as the first place new money appears; the same roof at the old multiple.

## Application Evidence

**Price versus income, named series.** Census / HUD median sales price of **new** houses, 1971: **$25,200**. Census median **family** income, 1971: about **$10,290** (P60-85). Ratio on the order of **2.5**. Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies: 2024 nationwide existing single-family median about **5.0 times** median household income, tying the 2005 record. Scope: those series are not identical (new vs existing; family vs household). The direction of the wedge is the point. Do not treat a chart compilation ("WTF Happened in 1971") as a primary source.

**Tax expenditure scale.** JCT scores mortgage-interest deduction and the principal-residence gain exclusion in the **hundreds of billions** over five-year windows (homeownership tax expenditures dominate rental ones). Scope-label the JCT year.

Zoning supply walls ([pla-074](exclusionary-zoning-housing-wall.md)) and foreclosure process ([pla-008](secured-credit-foreclosure.md)) are co-mechanisms. This page is the monetary and tax first-receiver half.
