# Consigned inventory routed as a civil business dispute

When high-value goods sit with a dealer, franchisee, or successor under a consignment or inventory arrangement, a fight over who may keep or sell them is treated as contract, conversion, and the UCC. Parallel pressure from outsiders — public campaigns, process-serving theatrics, residential picketing — is more readily charged as stalking, trespass, or targeted picketing. The property dispute stays civil; the noise around it can go criminal.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is that retention, sale, or freeze of consigned goods is litigated as a business dispute: breach, conversion, replevin, accounting, franchise termination, sometimes civil RICO or defamation countersuits. The parties who obtain that routing are firms, franchisors, and successors with counsel, arbitration clauses, and the ability to characterize the goods as collateral, store assets, or a private contract problem. The consignor who wants the police to "get my stuff back" is typically told it is civil.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A stranger who walks out of the same store with a box of unsold consigned goods is a theft defendant. An employee who pockets the till is on the criminal track ([pla-007](employee-theft-criminal-track.md)). A YouTuber, process server, or protestor who tries to force the issue in public is exposed to trespass, disorderly conduct, stalking, and targeted-picketing statutes. The closed analog is criminal conversion of the inventory itself when the alleged converter is a corporate successor or franchise network.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Conversion / replevin / detinue | Civil recovery of goods or value; no arrest | State tort and replevin statutes |
| UCC consignment | Many consignments are treated as Article 9 security interests; priority fights against other creditors | UCC §§ 9-102, 9-319 |
| UCC secured-party remedies | Repossession, disposition, deficiency after default | UCC Article 9 Part 6 |
| Franchise / contract / arbitration | Internalizes the dispute; TROs and gag provisions can limit public comment | Franchise agreements; FAA |
| Civil RICO / defamation countersuits | Raise the cost of public accusation | 18 U.S.C. § 1964; state defamation |
| Stalking, trespass, targeted picketing | Criminal overlay on advocacy and service of process around the civil dispute | State penal codes |

UCC § 9-319 can give a consignee rights in the goods as against the consignor's unsecured creditors if the consignment is an Article 9 consignment and the consignor did not perfect. That filing/perfection filter is invisible to a family that signed a store-level consignment form.

## Routing

Enabled: civil litigation, mediation, arbitration, TROs. Closed: treating successor retention of identified consigned goods as theft when the actors are in a commercial chain.

Procedural features:

- Police "civil matter" declination at the scene.
- Arbitration and forum clauses pull the consignor away from local courts.
- Cost of civil counsel vs. value of remaining inventory, plus incomplete records after a franchise collapse.
- Criminal statutes aimed at *how* outsiders apply pressure, not at *whether* the goods were converted.

## Application Evidence

This starter entry is qualitative at the pattern level and uses one recent illustration. It does not find liability.

**Illustration (facts alleged and disputed): Bricks & Minifigs / Mansell collection, Oregon, 2023–2026.**

Ed Mansell's LEGO Star Wars collection was placed on consignment with a Bricks & Minifigs franchise in the Salem-Keizer, Oregon area in 2023. Reported values range from about $95,000–$100,000 (corporate/later journalistic estimates) to about $200,000 (family and early reporting). Bryan Mansell has said he received about $15,000 in payments; corporate later alleged higher recorded sales. In November 2024 the franchisees reported insolvency (on the order of $200,000 in royalties, lease, and vendor debts). Corporate transferred the store to new franchisees. What inventory remained, who was told it was consigned, and whether return was offered are disputed.

When Mansell asked police to intervene, they treated it as civil. Public investigation by YouTuber "Reckless Ben" (Benjamin Schneider) produced viral videos, a corporate TRO/injunction limiting commentary, a civil RICO-style suit against advocates, trespass/disorderly-conduct exposure at corporate offices, and later stalking/trespass/targeted-picketing charges around a franchisee's residence. Former franchisees sued corporate for, among other things, conversion and seizure of store assets. Corporate has denied responsibility for a store-level consignment it says was unauthorized. The Keizer unit was later closed. Multiple civil cases were pending as of mid-2026. Value, remaining inventory, and bookkeeping are all contested.

The routing point does not require picking a winner. Inventory and franchise fights moved through contract, conversion, arbitration, and TROs. Police declined a theft frame against the commercial parties. Criminal energy concentrated on the public campaign.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 7 (Individual Domain).** Consigned goods remain, in the consignor's understanding, the consignor's domain. Civil routing treats that domain as a contract residual.
- **Rule 13 (Subject or object).** The commercial parties remain subjects in civil court. The outsider who applies public pressure is recast as a threat to be charged.
- **Rule 16 (Conflict Resolution).** The system imposes a commercial-process outcome and treats relationship repair (return the boxes, account for sales) as optional pending litigation.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** "It's a civil matter" is the hedge that decides the property question at the scene. Criminal statutes then attach to the advocacy, not to the inventory.
- **Rule 25 (Justice: restorative or punitive).** Restoration of identified goods would be the high-law analog. The observed mix is delayed civil process plus punitive overlay on the noisier, less-resourced advocates.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
