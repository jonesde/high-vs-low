# Eminent domain transferring land to designated consolidators

The Fifth Amendment allows taking private property for "public use" with just compensation. Courts have read that to include transfer to a private redeveloper for expected tax and jobs (*Kelo*) and, much longer, transfer to a private common carrier. The Natural Gas Act lets a certificate holder condemn land it cannot buy. The occupant has the land. The designated taker has the statute. Compensation is a legally set price, not a voluntary "no."

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is forced transfer of a specific parcel to a named project or franchisee. The parties who obtain it are cities and their development corporations, FERC-certificated pipelines, railroads, and other private delegates of the taking power. Occupants who would have refused the sale take the residual: money the court calls just, without the land.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A voluntary sale. A taking only for a government-held road, courthouse, or military post that remains in public ownership. *Kelo*'s dissenters named that analog and lost 5–4. NGA § 717f(h) still lets a private gas company stand in the United States' shoes.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Takings Clause | Public use plus just compensation as the limit, not a ban on private transferees | U.S. Const. amend. V |
| *Kelo* | Economic development for a private plan can be public use | *Kelo v. City of New London*, 545 U.S. 469 (2005) |
| *Berman* / *Midkiff* | Earlier public-purpose expansions (urban renewal; breaking a land oligopoly) | 348 U.S. 26 (1954); 467 U.S. 229 (1984) |
| NGA certificate condemnation | Holder of a certificate of public convenience and necessity may condemn | [15 U.S.C. § 717f(h)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/717f) |
| State post-*Kelo* statutes | Many states narrowed economic-development takings; the federal constitutional floor remains *Kelo* | State codes (uneven) |

**First receiver:** the condemnor's designee — NLDC in *Kelo*; the pipeline company under § 717f(h). **Delayed end:** the occupant, paid after the fact, often after the project's public benefits fail to appear (Fort Trumbull's promised Pfizer-adjacent development did not arrive as sold).

## Routing

Enabled: condemnation petition after a public-use or certificate finding; just-compensation trial as the only remaining fight. Closed: a veto by the occupant; a constitutional rule that "public use" means use by the public.

NGA condemnation has existed for private gas companies since 1947. *Kelo* is the economic-development expansion. Both are legal interposition between a person and a specific piece of earth.

## Application Evidence

*Kelo*: New London condemned non-blighted homes for a comprehensive plan expected to raise taxes and jobs, transferring land to a private development corporation. The Court held that expected public benefits can be public use, without "reasonable certainty" that benefits will accrue.

§ 717f(h): if a certificate holder cannot acquire by contract, "it may acquire the same by the exercise of the right of eminent domain." The occupant is not negotiating with a peer. The certificate is the ranking.

Post-*Kelo* state reforms are evidence that the federal floor was recognized as a transfer machine; they do not repeal NGA condemnation or the Fifth Amendment as read in *Kelo*.
