# Clergy privilege and discovery walls

Every state recognizes a clergy-penitent (or clergy-communicant) privilege: confidential spiritual communications are not ordinary evidence. Church-autonomy doctrine ([pla-049](church-autonomy-ecclesiastical-abstention.md)) is then used to resist discovery of personnel files, "canonical" archives, and internal discipline records in abuse and fraud suits. Mandatory-reporting statutes sometimes except clergy. The analog is a school, hospital, or company: the personnel file, the complaint log, and the transfer memo are discoverable in a negligent-supervision case.

This is the mechanism by which **documented harm is kept off the public docket** under the guise of religious liberty. The original liberty was belief, expression, and receipt. The victim's speech and suit are what the wall closes.

This page does not retry every allegation. Grand-jury reports and independent reviews are **documents of routing**, not library verdicts of each named person.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is missing paper. The parties who obtain it are religious organizations that can caption records as penitential, canonical, or ecclesiastical. Injured members and children take the residual: delayed suits, lost files, and statutes of limitation that run while the archive stays closed.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Attorney-client privilege (narrower; crime-fraud exception). Physician-patient (often pierced for child harm). Secular negligent-supervision discovery. The closed analog is a diocese or congregation producing the same transfer-and-complaint file a school district would produce.

**Other complex:** captured evidence ([pla-046](captured-evidence-associations.md)) — who mints the official record. Government-platform speech ([pla-047](government-platform-speech.md)) — closing dissent. Here the institution closes its **own** paper.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Clergy-penitent privilege | Confidential spiritual communications excluded from evidence | State evidence codes (e.g. Cal. Evid. Code §§ 1030–1034; similar in all states) |
| Church autonomy / abstention | First Amendment objections to discovering internal discipline | [pla-049](church-autonomy-ecclesiastical-abstention.md); *Watson* line |
| Mandatory-reporting exceptions | Some states except clergy from child-abuse reporting | State penal / child-protection codes (dated by state) |
| Sealing / in camera review | Courts sometimes review church files privately rather than produce them | State and federal discovery practice |

The two-witness rule used in some Jehovah's Witnesses congregational procedures is **internal policy**, not a statute. It becomes legally effective when archives stay out of court. Label it as alleged institutional practice.

## Routing

Enabled: privilege log; autonomy motion; reporting exception; bankruptcy stay ([pla-056](religious-mass-tort-bankruptcy.md)). Closed: ordinary personnel-file discovery; a crime-fraud-style pierce of penitential communications used to conceal ongoing harm.

## Application Evidence

Pennsylvania 40th Statewide Investigating Grand Jury report (released 2018): more than **300** predator priests, more than **1,000** child victims, in **six** dioceses (all in the state except Philadelphia and Altoona-Johnstown, which had earlier reports). The report describes bishops returning offenders to ministry after "treatment" and aiming to avoid publicity and liability. Scope: those dioceses, the grand jury's document set, those findings as a **state document**.

Southern Baptist Convention Guidepost Solutions report (May 2022): independent investigation of the SBC Executive Committee's handling of abuse, including resistance to a database of offenders. Scope: that report, that convention's national bodies.

Diocesan "secret archives" (canon law) and JW congregational files appear in discovery fights as the **things the privilege and autonomy motions are about**. Do not treat a failed motion in one case as a national rule.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 7 (Individual Domain).** The child's body is a domain the archive wall keeps off the docket.
- **Rule 9 (Who defines harm).** The institution defines the file as penitential; the injured person does not define the record.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** Privilege plus autonomy become the law of the file.
- **Rule 36 (Treatment of the Least).** Child claimants without the file are the least.
- **Rule 40 (Safeguards: principled or procedural).** Privilege is a procedural hole, not a principle that concealment preserves anyone's belief.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
