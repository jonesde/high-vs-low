# Zoning and code barriers that protect incumbents

The same zoning map, building code, and nuisance ordinance can grandfather an existing use, grant it a variance, or leave it unenforced, while a new or lower-resource entrant is denied, delayed, or ticketed. Preferential application here is mostly local and discretionary. The instruments are public; the split is in who can staff the process.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is protection of incumbent land uses and business locations: continued operation of nonconforming uses, successful variances and conditional-use permits, delayed or declined enforcement against established actors, and exclusion of competing housing or businesses. The parties who obtain it are existing owners, neighborhood groups with time to appear at hearings, and businesses that can hire land-use counsel. New entrants, extra dwelling units, home-based businesses, and lower-resource operators take the strict reading of the same text.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A new daycare, additional dwelling unit, food truck, or repair shop must prove it fits the zone, win a conditional use, or lose. An incumbent in the same building often holds a nonconforming-use right, a previously granted variance, or a relationship with inspectors that yields warnings instead of shutdowns. The analog conduct is operating a use on land. The closed path is doing that as a newcomer without political or legal capacity.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Zoning ordinance (Euclidean and later forms) | Separates uses; criminal or civil penalties for violation | Local codes; *Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty*, 272 U.S. 365 (1926) |
| Conditional use / special exception | Discretionary permission inside a zone | Local codes |
| Variance (use and area) | Escape hatch for hardship; often used by those who can show up and hire experts | Local codes; state enabling acts |
| Nonconforming-use (grandfather) protections | Legal continuation of uses that new rules would forbid | Local codes; state statutes |
| Nuisance and property-maintenance enforcement | Selective tickets, liens, and closures | Local codes; state nuisance statutes |
| Hearing and notice procedures | Time, expertise, and repeated appearances as the real filter | Local procedure |

## Routing

Enabled: administrative permission and non-enforcement for incumbents; civil or quasi-criminal enforcement against new or politically weak uses. Closed: as-of-right entry for the newcomer under the same use category the incumbent already occupies, or even-handed inspection.

Procedural features:

- Burden on the applicant for a variance or conditional use; neighbors who organize can block.
- Nonconforming-use law protects yesterday's use against today's rule.
- Code enforcement resources are finite. Inspectors respond to complaints. Complaints track who is disliked or who is new, not only who is dangerous.
- Local variation is the point. There is no single federal statistic that measures "preferential zoning" nationwide.

## Application Evidence

This starter entry is qualitative and doctrinal. Local variation is the evidence type, not a defect.

**Exclusionary zoning as incumbent protection.** Standard accounts (including Century Foundation overviews of exclusionary zoning) describe lot-size, density, and use rules that keep lower-cost housing and some businesses out of jurisdictions that already have housing wealth. The beneficiaries are existing owners. The constrained parties are would-be residents and new operators. That is preferential application of land-use power even when the ordinance is facially general.

**Discretionary permits.** Variance and conditional-use practice is inherently particular. Studies of local boards repeatedly find that outcomes track organization, representation, and "character of the neighborhood" arguments — that is, incumbent preference — more than a mechanical reading of hardship criteria. This page does not invent a national grant-rate table; it flags the mechanism.

**Selective enforcement.** Nuisance and property-maintenance codes can be applied to shut a new use quickly (unlicensed home business, extra occupancy, sidewalk activity) while an established violation next door continues. Complaint-driven enforcement plus cost of cure is the same filter as in [civil-asset-forfeiture.md](civil-asset-forfeiture.md): the party who cannot staff the process loses.

Treat any single-city anecdote as illustration, not as a national rate.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 2 (Power hierarchy: natural or equal?).** Zoning presents the incumbent map as the natural order of the block. Newcomers are the disturbance.
- **Rule 9 (Who defines harm).** Nuisance and "character" are defined by those already there. The newcomer's harm (exclusion) is not the official harm.
- **Rule 18 (Authority and obedience: chosen or imposed?).** Local land-use power is imposed on people who did not agree to that particular map.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** The text of the zone is general. Grandfathering, variance, and non-enforcement are the real law.
- **Rule 34 (Perfecting: elevation or exclusion?).** Alignment with the existing pattern is rewarded; difference is refined out.
- **Rule 36 (Treatment of the Least).** Lower-resource applicants and would-be residents are the ones the process screens out.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
