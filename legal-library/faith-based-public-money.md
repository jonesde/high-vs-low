# Faith-based public money

Blaine-era state no-aid clauses were a **barrier to receipt**: public money could not go to sectarian schools, written in an anti-Catholic settlement after the Civil War. Charitable Choice (PRWORA 1996, 42 U.S.C. § 604a) and the later faith-based executive structure invited religious providers into TANF and other social-service money. *Trinity Lutheran* (2017), *Espinoza* (2020), and *Carson v. Makin* (2022) then held that if a state funds private or generally available benefits, it may not exclude religious providers or religious schools. The analog is a storefront sect that never becomes a "provider," or a secular contractor under ordinary procurement without a Free Exercise entitlement to the stream — or, in the older settlement, a state that simply declined to fund religious instruction.

The inversion: a 19th-century barrier to receipt becomes a 21st-century **funding franchise** for the institutional school and the faith-based vendor. Catholic and other religious hospital systems as 501(c)(3) health providers sit on the same purse.

Civic religion — "under God" in the Pledge (1954), "In God We Trust" as the national motto (1956) — is compelled expression, a short overlay, not a second page. It is a barrier to belief and speech for the person who will not say it, while the institutional complex takes the public money.

This page does not decide whether a parent may choose a religious school. It names who is entitled to the check.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is public money at designated religious desks. The parties who obtain it are eligible faith-based providers, religious schools in funded programs, and hospital systems organized as religious charities. Taxpayers and unfavored or non-institutional believers take the residual: they fund a ministry they may not join, or they cannot get the analog benefit at a secular-only shop the state is no longer allowed to prefer.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A purely secular procurement under ordinary FAR-like rules ([pla-048](designated-cost-plus-contractor.md)). A state that offers no private-school aid at all (*Carson* still allows that). Unfunded storefront practice. Original Establishment no-aid (*Everson*'s wall language, later thinned). Native boarding-school contracts ([pla-051](contracted-native-conversion.md)) are the dark ancestor: public money to churches to compel belief. This page is public money to churches as **equal participants** and then as constitutionally required participants.

**Other complex:** funding-conditioned mandates ([pla-043](funding-conditioned-mandates.md)) — the purse as compulsion of the recipient. Here the purse is a franchise of the provider.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Charitable Choice | Religious organizations may receive TANF and related funds without stripping worship | [42 U.S.C. § 604a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/604a); PRWORA 1996 |
| Faith-based executive structure | White House and agency offices to place faith-based providers | Bush 2001 executive orders; later administrations' revisions |
| *Trinity Lutheran* | Generally available playground grant cannot exclude a church | *Trinity Lutheran Church of Columbia, Inc. v. Comer*, 582 U.S. 449 (2017) |
| *Espinoza* | Scholarship funds cannot exclude religious schools because of religious status | *Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue*, 591 U.S. 464 (2020) |
| *Carson* | Tuition assistance cannot exclude schools that will use the money for religious instruction | *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022) |
| Blaine-era no-aid clauses | State constitutional barriers to sectarian funding (the older receipt wall) | State constitutions; failed federal Blaine Amendment (1875) |
| Pledge / motto overlays | Compelled civic-religious expression | 4 U.S.C. § 4 ("under God," 1954); 36 U.S.C. § 302 (motto, 1956) |

*Locke v. Davey*, 540 U.S. 712 (2004), allowed a state to withhold scholarship money for **devotional theology degrees**. *Carson* does not overrule *Locke* by name; it narrows the space in which a state may refuse to fund religious **use**. Document that tension; do not pretend *Locke* is gone.

## Routing

Enabled: grant eligibility; Free Exercise suit to enter the stream; Charitable Choice contracting. Closed: a no-aid clause that keeps school money secular; a procurement that simply does not include churches.

Religious hospitals take Medicare/Medicaid as 501(c)(3)s and also invoke conscience statutes to decline procedures. That is purse-plus-refusal: public money without the analog hospital's full service set. Cross-link [pla-043](funding-conditioned-mandates.md); do not turn this page into clinical policy.

## Application Evidence

*Carson*'s holding is the school-money mechanism: Maine's town-tuitioning program could not limit payments to nonsectarian schools. *Espinoza* struck a Blaine-style no-aid application to scholarships. Charitable Choice is the 1996 social-service pipe. Religious hospital systems' share of nonprofit beds is a large 501(c)(3) application — cite AHA or CMS counts by year if used; do not invent a percentage.

Interior boarding-school appropriations ([pla-051](contracted-native-conversion.md), $23.3 billion FY2023 dollars, 1871–1969) are the earlier public-money-to-churches evidence. Same purse shape; opposite claim of liberty.

## Framework notes

Relevant distinction rules from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md):

- **Rule 8 (Response to "No").** A state's "no" to funding religious instruction is met with a Free Exercise command to pay.
- **Rule 18 (Authority: chosen or imposed?).** The funding franchise is imposed as the price of having any private-school aid.
- **Rule 22 (Legalism as Idol).** "Equal participation" becomes the law of the check.
- **Rule 33 (Dominion: stewardship or ownership).** Public money as a ministry's owned stream.
- **Rule 42 (Production: distributed or centralized).** Storefront belief is distributed; the eligible provider list is centralized.

Do not score this entry. The rules name the pattern; they do not replace the instruments or the evidence.
