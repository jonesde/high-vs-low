# RLUIPA and property-tax franchise

Ordinary land sits under local zoning ([pla-009](zoning-incumbent-protection.md)) and, if commercial, under the property-tax roll. The Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (2000) forbids state and local land-use rules that substantially burden religious exercise unless the government meets a compelling-interest / least-restrictive-means test. *Walz v. Tax Commission* (1970) upheld property-tax exemptions for worship. State codes then exempt churches — and often parking, parsonages, undeveloped expansion parcels, and mixed campus uses. Megachurch campuses, broadcast facilities, and school-plus-sanctuary tracts sit on that stack. The analog is a commercial campus of similar footprint, taxed and zoned as of right without a federal compelling-interest overlay.

This is the real-estate half of the recent explosion of religious institutions with commercial-adjacent operations.

This page does not decide whether a congregation should have a building. It names who skips the analog tax and the analog variance fight.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a preferred land track. The parties who obtain it are religious landowners who can plead a substantial burden. Neighboring commercial and residential uses, and secular nonprofits without RLUIPA, take the residual: ordinary zoning and ordinary millage.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A big-box store or office park on the same acreage. A secular private school seeking a special-use permit. [pla-009](zoning-incumbent-protection.md) incumbents who already hold variances — RLUIPA is a **federal thumb** on that local discretion when the applicant is religious. The closed analog is treating worship-adjacent commercial campus as commercial land.

RLUIPA's institutionalized-persons prong (prisons) is a different claimant: prisoners seeking ritual. That prong can protect individual belief and receipt. The land-use prong is the institutional franchise. Keep them distinct; this page is land.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| RLUIPA land-use | Substantial burden on religious land use needs compelling interest and least restrictive means | [42 U.S.C. §§ 2000cc et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000cc) |
| RLUIPA equal-terms / nondiscrimination | Religious assemblies may not be treated worse than secular assemblies | 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc(b) |
| *Walz* | Property-tax exemption for churches upheld | *Walz v. Tax Commission of City of New York*, 397 U.S. 664 (1970) |
| State worship exemptions | Statutory property-tax exemption, often including parking and related parcels | State revenue codes |
| *Boerne* (the hole RLUIPA fills) | RFRA cannot commandeer state land-use | *City of Boerne v. Flores*, 521 U.S. 507 (1997) |

RLUIPA was the land-and-prison answer to *Boerne*. Equal-terms litigation is how megachurch and storefront churches both punch through zoning that allows theaters or meeting halls but not churches — a real barrier-to-entry story **and** a franchise for large campuses. Document both. The preferential fact at campus scale is tax plus the compelling-interest overlay.

## Routing

Enabled: RLUIPA suit; tax-exempt parcel classification; special-use as of a federal right. Closed: ordinary variance; taxed commercial assessment of the same parking and classroom wings.

## Application Evidence

DOJ RLUIPA enforcement (housing-and-civil-rights reports) documents land-use cases; scope-label by DOJ year. *Walz* is the tax holding. Local assessment fights over whether a bookstore, cafe, gym, or unused acreage is "used for worship" are the commercial-adjacent application. Do not invent a national exempt-acreage total. Property-record case studies (named megachurch campuses) are illustrations; label assessed-value claims to the assessor's roll.

Prison RLUIPA (*Cutter v. Wilkinson*, 544 U.S. 709 (2005)) is individual-exercise evidence, not this page's land franchise. One sentence is enough.
