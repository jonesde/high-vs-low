# Compulsory attendance

Massachusetts' 1852 Act Concerning the Attendance of Children at School required children aged 8–14 to attend a designated school for a minimum term; parents who refused faced fines. By 1918 every state had a compulsory-attendance statute. Truancy and "educational neglect" are the civil or criminal residual. *Meyer v. Nebraska*, 262 U.S. 390 (1923), and *Pierce v. Society of Sisters*, 268 U.S. 510 (1925), preserved a paid or parental analog (language instruction; a private school instead of the state's). They did not restore family instruction **without an attendance ledger**. The analog is a family that instructs without a statutory exception. [pla-051](contracted-native-conversion.md) is the custody extreme of the same compulsion: the child's days taken by contract.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is that the child's time is a counted public resource. The parties who obtain it are state education agencies and local districts. Families take the residual: enroll, claim a statutory exception, or face truancy process.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Family instruction as of right, with no days ledger. *Pierce* private school as a **paid** analog that still counts days. *Yoder* ([pla-057](faith-healing-child-exemptions.md)) as a religious exemption after eighth grade — a crack, not the default. The closed analog is instruction at home that is not a "school" in the statute.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** The law is sold as literacy. The enforcement residual is **process against the parent** (fines, loss of custody in the 1852 model, modern truancy court), not a showing that the analog child cannot read.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative enrollment and day-count; civil or criminal truancy. Closed: family instruction without a statutory school.

## Application Evidence

The 1852 act and the 1918 nationwide map are the mechanism. Truancy-court and "educational neglect" dockets are application; cite a named state year rather than a national conviction rate.

Boarding-school custody ([pla-051](contracted-native-conversion.md)) is the same compulsion with the child physically taken. Homeschool statutes ([pla-082](homeschool-umbrella-capture.md)) are the later exception that still often requires notice, hours, or a covering school.
