# Ministerial exception

Title VII and the ADA generally forbid employment discrimination and retaliation. *Hosanna-Tabor* (2012) held that the Religion Clauses bar such suits when the employee is a "minister." *Our Lady of Guadalupe* (2020) applied that bar to lay Catholic-school teachers who taught religion and modeled the faith, without requiring ordination or a clerical title. Title VII already excepts religious organizations from certain religion-based hiring rules (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1). The ministerial exception is broader: it can close **disability, sex, age, and retaliation** claims for anyone a court tags as a minister.

The analog is a public- or secular-school teacher, or a church janitor in a circuit that still treats that job as non-ministerial. The labor franchise is the power to fire without a civil-rights docket.

This page does not decide who a church should hire. It names who loses the statute.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a hole in employment law at religious employers. The parties who obtain it are churches, religious schools, and other religious organizations. Workers who perform religious functions — an expanding set — take the residual.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A secular private-school teacher with an ADA retaliation claim. A public-school teacher under Title VII. Ordinary at-will employment, which still leaves statutory civil-rights overlays. The closed analog is a merits hearing on why this teacher was fired.

**Other complex:** unpaid wages on the civil track ([pla-001](unpaid-wages-civil-track.md)) — institutions already sit on a softer labor track; this page removes even that for "ministers." Health-complex licensing monopoly ([pla-044](licensing-monopoly-on-the-analog.md)) is a guild gate; this is a church gate on the same worker.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| *Hosanna-Tabor* | Religion Clauses bar employment-discrimination suits by ministers | *Hosanna-Tabor Evangelical Lutheran Church and School v. EEOC*, 565 U.S. 171 (2012) |
| *Our Lady of Guadalupe* | Lay teachers who perform vital religious duties are ministers | *Our Lady of Guadalupe School v. Morrissey-Berru*, 591 U.S. 728 (2020) |
| Title VII religious-org exemption | Statutory cousin: religion-based hiring by religious employers | [42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000e-1) |
| ADA | The statute *Hosanna-Tabor* held could not be used by a called teacher | 42 U.S.C. §§ 12101 et seq. |

*Hosanna-Tabor* listed four considerations (title, substance, own use of the title, religious functions). *Our Lady* said function can suffice. Lower courts have applied the exception to a drama teacher, a kosher inspector, principals, and others. That expansion is the preferential application.

## Routing

Enabled: motion to dismiss on ministerial exception; no merits on the statutory claim. Closed: EEOC or private Title VII/ADA action for the tagged employee.

Wage-and-hour claims sometimes survive and sometimes do not; circuit split. Do not treat FLSA as uniformly closed. The core holding is discrimination/retaliation.

## Application Evidence

*Hosanna-Tabor*: called teacher, narcolepsy, ADA retaliation — dismissed. *Our Lady*: two lay Catholic-school teachers, age and disability claims — dismissed. Those facts are the Court's own application.

OnLabor and circuit surveys (2020s) document expansion beyond clergy. Scope-label by circuit. The preferential enabler is **who counts as a minister**, not a count of all religious employees in the United States.
