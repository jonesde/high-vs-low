# Prior appropriation and federal Reclamation storage

In prior-appropriation states, water is allocated by **first beneficial use**, not by riparian adjacency. A senior right takes the stream in drought before a junior right, a later homesteader, or an instream use that was never filed. The Reclamation Act of 1902 then stored and delivered federal project water under contracts that sit on top of — and sometimes reshape — those state rights. The analog is riparian reasonable use, or unappropriated flow as a commons.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a time-ranked claim on a mortal necessity. The parties who obtain it are senior appropriators and Reclamation contractors (irrigation districts, later municipal and hydropower contractors). Junior users, tribes without quantified reserved rights, and later arrivals take the residual when the river is short.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Riparian reasonable use (eastern common law): land on the stream shares the flow; no "first in time, first in right" that can zero out a neighbor. Unappropriated water as a public trust or instream flow. Those analogs are closed west of the 100th meridian by state codes, with federal reserved rights (*Winters*) as a limited overlay that still must be litigated into wet water.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Prior-appropriation codes | Diversion plus beneficial use plus priority date; "use it or lose it" | State water codes (e.g. Colorado, Wyoming, Idaho, Utah, Nevada) |
| Reclamation Act of 1902 | Federal storage and delivery; repayment contracts; acreage limits (later amended) | Act of June 17, 1902; 43 U.S.C. § 371 et seq. |
| Federal reserved rights | Implied reservation of water for federal lands and tribes as of the reservation date | *Winters v. United States*, 207 U.S. 564 (1908) |
| McCarran Amendment | State-court general stream adjudications may join the United States | 43 U.S.C. § 666 |

**First receiver:** the senior priority and the Reclamation contractor with a stored-water contract. **Delayed end:** junior irrigators, later cities, and unmet reserved rights.

## Routing

Enabled: administrative permits, ditch-company shares, Reclamation contracts, general stream adjudications. Closed: equal sharing of a short river among all adjacent users; a new arrival taking water without a priority.

Paper water vs. wet water is the application filter: a junior with a permit still stands behind a senior. Reclamation storage can firm some juniors and still rank contractors ahead of those outside the project.

## Application Evidence

This entry is structural. Priority dates and Reclamation contract queues are the evidence. Drought administration in prior-appropriation states (Colorado call, California curtailment of post-1914 rights, 2020s Colorado River shortage guidelines) shows seniors and contractors taking water while juniors are curtailed. That is first-in-time as legal interposition, not as a hydrologic necessity.

The 1902 Act's original 160-acre limit was a homestead-scale gesture. Later reclamation practice and contract amendments concentrated delivery in districts that could repay. Name that as application, not as a single acreage table unless sourced.
