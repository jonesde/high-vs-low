# Historical technique privilege

People with capital and skill have long been legally barred from using a technique the incumbent already had. Three citable episodes, one through-line: **who may print**, **who may export a machine or an artisan**, **who may attach a device to a network**. Modern repair locks, export controls, and platform APIs are the same shape.

This is not a history essay. It is three documented privileges.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a legal monopoly on a technique: printing, textile-machine building, or connecting equipment to the telephone network. The parties who obtain it are the chartered company, the metropole, or the carrier. Outsiders with presses, capital, or a better device are the closed class.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Unlicensed practice by an outsider who has the press, the workshop, or the acoustic coupler. The analog is ordinary use of a owned machine on a paid service. The closed path is doing that without the privilege.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| Instrument | Role in the outcome | Citation |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| Stationers' Company charter and press licensing | Crown-chartered company (1557) plus Licensing of the Press Act 1662: who may print, and what | Royal Charter 1557; 14 Cha. 2 c. 33 (1662) |
| British bans on exporting textile machinery and artisan emigration | Workshops and skilled workers in Britain still could not take the technique out | Tools Act 1781, 21 Geo. 3 c. 37; artisan-emigration bans; substantially repealed by 5 Geo. 4 c. 97 (1824); remaining machinery-export restrictions lifted in stages through 1843 |
| AT&T tariffs forbidding customer attachments | Paid telephone service; customer-owned devices still prohibited | FCC Tariff No. 132 and predecessors |
| *Hush-a-Phone* | D.C. Circuit: a cup on the handset is the subscriber's private use | *Hush-a-Phone Corp. v. United States*, 238 F.2d 266 (D.C. Cir. 1956) |
| *Carterfone* | FCC: carrier may not prohibit interconnection of customer devices that do not harm the network | *Use of the Carterfone Device in Message Toll Telephone Service*, 13 F.C.C.2d 420 (1968) |
| FCC Part 68 | Successor registration franchise: terminal equipment may attach if it meets designated technical criteria | 47 C.F.R. Part 68 (1975 onward); Administrative Council for Terminal Attachments |

## Routing

Enabled: charter, statute, or tariff as the exclusive practice rule; penalties for unlicensed printing, export, or attachment. Closed: as-of-right use of the outsider's own press, machine, or coupler on a paid service.

Procedural features:

- Stationers combined copyright-ancestor privileges with search and seizure of unlicensed presses.
- Machinery bans treated the artisan's body as an export-controlled vessel for the technique (see [pla-016](technical-data-export-controls.md) deemed export).
- Telephone tariffs made attachment a tariff violation until FCC/court process reopened it.

## Application Evidence

**Print.** The Stationers' charter and the 1662 Licensing Act are the citable privilege: printing is not what you do with a press you own; it is what the company and the licensor allow. US copyright later secularized part of that privilege; the exclusive-right shape remained.

**Machines.** Britain's prohibition on exporting specified textile machinery (Tools Act 1781, 21 Geo. 3 c. 37) and on the emigration of artisans is the citable "you have the workshop, you may not transfer the technique." Independent US and continental mills with capital still needed smuggled machines and people. Repeal in stages through 5 Geo. 4 c. 97 (1824) and 1843 is evidence that the restriction was legal, not technical.

**Networks.** For decades AT&T's tariffs forbade "any equipment, apparatus, circuit or device not furnished by the Telephone Company." Subscribers paid for service. Hush-a-Phone (a plastic cup) and Carterfone (a radio patch) were still illegal attachments until 1956 and 1968. *Carterfone* is the documented crack: harm to the network, not the carrier's product line, is the limit. **Part 68** (1975) implemented that crack as a **registration franchise**: the analog coupler still needs a designated path. Parts-pairing, export controls, and platform APIs ([pla-003](manufacturer-repair-control.md), [pla-016](technical-data-export-controls.md), [pla-012](platform-safe-harbors.md)) are the live descendants. This page remains the ancestor so [pla-013](digital-license-not-sale.md)–[pla-024](constrained-open-licenses.md) is a history, not a 21st-century accident. Building-code occupancy of a house you have the skill to raise is the lumber cousin ([pla-075](building-code-hud-manufactured-floor.md)).
