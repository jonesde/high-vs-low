# AG-only charitable standing

A private trust can be enforced by beneficiaries. A charitable trust's beneficiaries are "indefinite" — the public, the poor, the students. The common-law and statutory default is that **the attorney general** sues in parens patriae. Donors usually lack standing once the deduction is taken; UPMIFA's drafters dropped an express donor-standing clause and said the AG remains protector of donor intent and of the public's interest in the funds. A few states (Kansas, Iowa, North Carolina among them) give donors a statute — label them as exceptions. Analog contract third-party beneficiaries can sue. Analog living poor who were supposed to be fed generally cannot.

Controllers: the AG's charity bureau (often small), trustees, and boards who know the default is **non-action**. This is how harm to claimed beneficiaries is procedurally closed.

This page does not decide any particular AG's diligence. It names who has a courthouse.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a missing plaintiff. The parties who obtain it are charities and trustees. Intended beneficiaries and most donors take the residual: no docket.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A remainder beneficiary of a family trust. A donee-beneficiary of a contract. Special-interest standing in a minority of charitable cases (a named school; a parishioner with a distinct injury) — a crack, not a general docket. The closed analog is the person the gift described walking into court.

**Other complex:** church autonomy ([pla-049](church-autonomy-ecclesiastical-abstention.md)) and qualified immunity ([pla-045](qualified-immunity.md)) — other inside tracks that are not a jury. Religious mass-tort bankruptcy ([pla-056](religious-mass-tort-bankruptcy.md)) is a different close: claims exist, then they are stayed.

| UTC enforcement | Settlor, AG, and (narrowly) others as enacted | Uniform Trust Code § 405 cmt. / enforcement provisions |

Philanthropy Roundtable's 50-state donor-intent survey (2024) is a map of **exceptions**, not a claim that standing is general. Use it as scope-labeled inventory.

## Routing

Enabled: AG suit (rare); special-interest suit (narrow); donor suit in exception states. Closed: ordinary beneficiary enforcement; a private right of action for the analog poor.

Uniform Supervision acts add registration and reports. They do not give the beneficiary a complaint form. Non-action by the AG is the operating law of most restricted gifts.

## Application Evidence

UPMIFA's own prefatory note is the mechanism: the AG, not the donor or the student, is the protector. Brody and other standing literature document dismissals for lack of standing — cite named cases, do not invent a national win-rate.

Missouri and similar decisions committing standing "exclusively" to the AG are application. NC/KS/IA donor-standing statutes are the labeled minority track.
