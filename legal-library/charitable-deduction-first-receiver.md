# Charitable deduction as first-receiver tax expenditure

26 U.S.C. § 170 lets an itemizing taxpayer deduct a "charitable contribution" paid during the year to a qualifying organization. Estate and gift tax have parallel deductions (§§ 2055, 2522). The analog gift — cash to a named neighbor, a mutual-aid circle without an EIN, a person who actually needs the money — is not deductible. The deduction's dollar value is the gift times the donor's marginal rate, so the same hundred dollars costs a high-bracket donor less after tax than it costs a non-itemizer. That is a **tax-expenditure first-receiver** ([pla-029](fiat-first-receiver-impulse.md)): public revenue is forgone first at the designated desk.

Controllers are the Code and the qualifying org (the EIN that can receive the deductible wire). The people the gift was supposed to help are not parties to the deduction.

This page does not decide whether deductibility "increases giving." It names who gets the tax benefit, and when.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a public subsidy of gifts to franchisees, timed at contribution, not at expenditure on a person. The parties who obtain it are itemizers and the organizations in § 170(c). Non-itemizers and informal recipients take the residual: no deduction, and often no gift because the deductible channel crowded the analog.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A birthday gift, a rent payment for a cousin, a church poor-box in cash that never becomes a § 170 receipt. Non-itemizers (standard deduction) get $0 tax expenditure for the same 501(c)(3) check. The closed analog is treating a gift to a living person as the charitable event.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** deduction is complete when the DAF or foundation receives the assets ([pla-068](donor-advised-funds-no-payout.md), [pla-067](private-foundation-perpetual-control.md)); the claimed beneficiary may wait years or forever. The Code scores the donor, not the meal.

§ 170(c) defines the qualifying donee. Informal mutual aid is not on that list ([pla-062](501c3-franchise-vs-mutual-aid.md)).

## Routing

Enabled: itemized deduction; substantiation receipts from the org. Closed: deduction for a gift to a natural person; a tax benefit timed to the beneficiary's receipt.

## Application Evidence

JCT and Treasury tax-expenditure budgets score the charitable deduction by year — cite the table, do not invent a "percent of giving caused by the deduction." The rate-schedule point is structural: a 37% itemizer and a non-itemizer are not equal after tax on the same check.

DAF and private-foundation contributions count for § 170 when the vehicle is funded, not when a grant reaches an operating charity. That timing is the first-receiver overlay.
