# GENIUS Act permitted payment-stablecoin issuance

The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act (signed July 18, 2025) makes it unlawful for anyone other than a **permitted payment stablecoin issuer** to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States. Permitted issuers must hold one-to-one reserves in a short list of assets (dollars, short-term Treasuries, deposits at regulated institutions, and, as the Act allows, central-bank money). They may not pay interest on the stablecoin. That is a new licensed ticket plus a legally required bid for public debt. It is not the same franchise as Federal Reserve notes. It is the same *shape*: issue privilege, designated first receivers, delayed public.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is a closed issuer class and a standing demand for T-bills and reserve-like assets. The parties who obtain the issue privilege are IDI subsidiaries, OCC-approved federal qualified issuers (including nonbanks and uninsured national banks), and state-qualified issuers. OCC conditionally granted national trust-bank charters to Circle, Paxos, and others in December 2025. Holders of the coin get a non-interest-bearing claim. Unlicensed issuers are closed.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

Anyone issuing a redeemable ticket against metal, goods, or a mixed portfolio; an interest-bearing private money; an unlicensed token that trades as a dollar substitute. The Act's § 3(a) (12 U.S.C. § 5902) forbids that analog inside the United States once the offer-and-sale prohibitions take effect (the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after final rules, with a three-year outer date for offering unlicensed coins).

| Trust-bank charters | OCC conditional national trust charters to nonbank issuers | OCC Dec. 2025 |

**First receiver:** the permitted issuer (seigniorage from float, since no interest is paid to holders) and the Treasury (forced bid for bills). **Delayed end:** the holder of a non-yielding dollar ticket while T-bill rates are positive; banks that lose deposits to non-interest stablecoins (the disintermediation side).

## Routing

Enabled: license plus reserve composition plus a prohibition on unlicensed issue. Closed: free issue of a dollar-like ticket; paying the holder for the float.

The page records both directions. Nonbank issuers with OCC charters can compete with banks for payment balances (Brookings, Mar. 2026). Banks and The Clearing House have asked Treasury to keep the no-interest rule tight so stablecoins do not substitute for deposits. That fight is evidence of a **new first-receiver slot**, not of a finished equilibrium.

Do not equate GENIUS with the Fed. Fed notes are legal tender ([pla-027](legal-tender-forced-acceptance.md)). GENIUS coins are licensed private (or IDI-subsidiary) tickets that must warehouse public debt. The centralizing mechanism here is the **license plus the T-bill/reserve channel**.

## Application Evidence

Congress.gov text of S.1582: "It shall be unlawful for any person other than a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States." That sentence is the franchise.

Reserve composition, as described in contemporaneous firm memos: US dollars, Fed notes, funds at insured/regulated depositories, short-term Treasuries and Treasury-backed reverse repo, money-market funds. The list is the legally required first use of the proceeds of issue.

No-interest: the float stays with the issuer (and, via T-bills, with the Treasury). Holders are the delayed end relative to bill rates.

Rulemaking through 2026 is the live application layer. Cite the tracker; do not freeze a proposed capital number as law.
