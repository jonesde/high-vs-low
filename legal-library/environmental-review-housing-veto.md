# Environmental-review housing veto

The National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA, 1970) requires federal agencies to study environmental effects before major actions. California's CEQA (1970) and other state NEPA-cousins attach similar study, comment, and suit rights to **private** housing when a discretionary permit is required. Incumbent houses and plants are grandfathered; the next infill project takes the document, the delay, and the lawsuit. This page is **housing-supply veto**. It is not a full environmental-permitting wave (incumbent plants versus new facilities remains a growth domain). The analog is infill housing on the same calendar as an incumbent nonconforming use.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is that new dwellings are slower and more suable than the stock already on the ground. The parties who obtain it are neighbors, incumbents, and counsel who can staff a CEQA/NEPA record. Would-be residents take the residual: fewer units, later.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

As-of-right ministerial housing that does not trigger review. An incumbent factory or house that continues without a new EIR. The closed analog is treating additional dwellings as continuation of an existing residential use.

**Hypocrisy (two tracks):** Environmental review is sold as protection of the analog person's air and water. On housing, the same process is a **veto held by those already housed**. Fischel's 1970s growth-control wave ([pla-074](exclusionary-zoning-housing-wall.md)) sits in the same decade as NEPA and CEQA.

| Ministerial / statutory exemptions | Partial analog: some housing is declared not discretionary | State housing-element and CEQA-exemption statutes (date-label) |

## Routing

Enabled: administrative review plus civil suit to stop or stall the next building. Closed: ministerial infill on the incumbent's calendar.

## Application Evidence

NEPA and CEQA's 1970 texts are the mechanism. Housing-element, builder's-remedy, and CEQA-exemption fights in California are the live application: the state later carved openings because the review was killing supply. Cite a named statute year for any exemption. Do not invent a statewide "percent of units killed" without a named study.

This is process as the wall, not a verdict on whether any particular habitat is real. Incumbent nonconforming industrial next to a stalled housing project is the illustration of grandfathering.
