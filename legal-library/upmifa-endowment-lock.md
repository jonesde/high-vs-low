# UPMIFA endowment lock

The Uniform Management of Institutional Funds Act (1972) and its successor UPMIFA (2006), enacted in almost every state, tell charities how to invest and spend "institutional funds." UPMIFA replaces a historic dollar-amount income rule with "prudence," and lists preservation of the fund's purchasing power among the factors. Boards adopt spending rates (often around 4–5% of a moving average) that are designed to let the corpus grow. Underwater endowments may still spend. Analog: a restricted gift that must be used for the named need. Cy-près and modification inside UPMIFA ([pla-063](cypres-institutional-preservation.md)) rewrite the restriction rather than return the money.

Controllers: boards and CIOs. The student, patient, or named poor person is not at the investment committee. University tuition-up while endowments grow is the loud illustration; do not invent an elasticity. Published spending policies plus the statute are the evidence.

This page does not decide the right spending rate. It names the default: **keep the pile**.

## Outcome / Goal

The practical result is legally prudent non-expenditure of most of the fund each year. The parties who obtain it are endowed institutions. Analog current beneficiaries take the spending slice.

## Contrast / Parallel Track

A private trust that distributes. A spend-down charity. A current-use restricted gift. The closed analog is a duty to spend the gift on the purpose in a short window, or to return what cannot be used.

## Formal Legal Instruments

| ---------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| UMIFA | Historic institutional-funds uniform act (appreciation as spendable) | UMIFA (1972) |
| UPMIFA | Prudence standard; spending; modification of restrictions | UPMIFA (2006) as enacted by states |
| Cy-près / deviation inside UPMIFA | Rewrite rather than return | UPMIFA § 6; [pla-063](cypres-institutional-preservation.md) |
| Donor standing (mostly absent) | UPMIFA dropped express donor standing | UPMIFA Prefatory Note; [pla-064](ag-only-charitable-standing.md) |

NCCUSL's prefatory note states the AG remains protector of donor intent **and of the public's interest in charitable funds**. That public interest is the sector's interest in the corpus.

## Routing

Enabled: board spending policy; prudent investment; modification with AG notice. Closed: mandatory current-use; analog beneficiary enforcement of a spend duty.

## Application Evidence

UPMIFA enactment in 49 states (and the District) is the diffusion — confirm current map at write; the uniform text is the mechanism. NACUBO endowment-study average spending rates (cite year) are application of the prudence default. Underwater-endowment spending authorized by UPMIFA is the 2008-crisis illustration: the statute was used to **keep spending without invading a historic dollar corpus rule**, i.e. to protect the institution's payout smoothness, not to empty the fund onto students.
